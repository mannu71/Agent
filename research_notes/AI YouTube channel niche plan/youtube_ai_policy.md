# YouTube Rules on AI-Generated, Faceless and AI-Voiced Content: Monetization in 2026 (as of Oct 3, 2026)

Source quality note: YouTube Help Center pages (support.google.com) were fetched directly and are treated as primary. TechCrunch was fetched and is reliable secondary. Many 2026 "explainer" blogs (creatorblade, outlierkit, air.io, bottlerocketcontent, flocker) are SEO-style aggregators. Their claims are used only where a better source corroborates them, or are flagged as unverified.

## 1. The July 2025 "inauthentic content" rename, the July 2026 clarification, and enforcement since

### Takeaway
On July 15, 2025, YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" in its YPP monetization policies. That was a rename and clarification, not a new ban on AI. On July 16, 2026, YouTube clarified the policy further into three named categories that cannot be monetized: (1) generic, repetitive or template-based content, (2) unsatisfying or off-putting content, and (3) AI personas on sensitive topics such as finance, legal and health. Enforcement got much heavier from December 2025 onward, with large channel terminations. A YPP threshold overhaul announced Aug 10, 2026 takes effect Feb 1, 2027.

### Cited Findings
- The YouTube Help Center's YPP monetization policies page says the "repetitious content" policy was renamed "inauthentic content", updated July 15, 2025, to clarify that it covers "content that is repetitive or mass-produced." — [YouTube Help: YPP monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Prohibited under inauthentic content: "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation across videos", and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Allowed: "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different", and "a series following a set of characters across episodes or a channel that does product reviews, but in which each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- The policy is applied channel-wide. Violations can demonetize the whole channel, not just one video. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- A dated timeline says YouTube stressed that the July 2025 change did not alter the rules for legitimate reaction, clip or compilation formats. — [AIR Media-Tech timeline](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline)
- **July 16, 2026 clarification:** YouTube Trust & Safety chief Matt Halprin announced three categories of inauthentic content that cannot be monetized, in a Creator Insider video. TechCrunch reported it on July 20, 2026. — [TechCrunch, Jul 20 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
  1. **Generic, repetitive or template-based content.** This covers AI-, CGI- or template-made videos with minimal variation, and even tutorials that just reproduce content already common on the platform. Halprin: "AI can actually allow people to make a lot of videos... very generic and don't really have a narrative arc." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
  2. **Unsatisfying or off-putting content.** This means emotionally manipulative, view-chasing material, for example an animal shown in distress and then "rescued." Halprin: "We've heard from our viewers that that's not something that they like. They find it off-putting." This applies whether or not AI was used. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [Yahoo Finance/Moneywise](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/off-putting-youtube-tighten-rules-104500810.html)
  3. **AI personas discussing sensitive topics**, meaning finance, legal, healthcare and medical. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- TechCrunch reports that high-quality AI-assisted content that adds creativity and originality stays eligible for YPP. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- An aggregator's reading of the July 2026 guidance: the trigger is that videos are interchangeable, not how many there are. Its example is "Top 10 Facts About [Country]" made 400 times by find-and-replace. It also says "faceless narration over stock or licensed footage", recurring characters, consistent formats and AI used for scripting or editing all remain monetizable, and that AI narration of research is allowed while presenting a fake credentialed expert is not. — [CreatorBlade](https://creatorblade.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026-stay-monetized) (secondary; it cites no primary link)
- One 2026 explainer claims inauthentic-content detection now runs at channel level rather than video by video. — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-cant-tell-a-directed-ai-film-from-a-bot-farm) (secondary; not confirmed by YouTube)
- CEO Neal Mohan's January 2026 annual letter named managing "AI slop" a top priority for 2026. — [CNBC, Jan 21 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/21/youtube-chief-says-managing-ai-slop-is-a-priority-for-2026-.html) (cited via OutlierKit; the CNBC page itself was not fetched)
- YouTube Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie has said YouTube welcomes creators using AI tools to improve storytelling, and that channels using AI remain eligible for monetization. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/) (secondary paraphrase)
- **YPP overhaul, the first since 2018, announced Aug 10, 2026 and effective Feb 1, 2027.** See section 4. — [YouTube Help: Changes to YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en); [Newsshooter, Aug 10 2026](https://www.newsshooter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-updates-its-partner-program/)
- Other 2025–26 monetization changes in the aggregator timeline (secondary): Mar 10, 2025, a human review layer was added to automated ad-suitability checks; Mar 2025, Shorts views count every play with no minimum watch time; May 2026, channel descriptions became directly enforceable under the Community Guidelines. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline)

### Inferences
- The legal text has barely changed since July 2025. What changed in 2026 is (a) enforcement intensity, including automated cluster-level detection, and (b) explicit categories. Category 3 matters a lot for a faceless AI channel: an AI "doctor," "CA" or "lawyer" persona giving health, finance or legal advice is a direct demonetization trigger, even when the facts are correct.
- Category 2 means "AI rescue" or outrage-bait storytelling formats are at risk whether or not they use AI.
- Status: the July 15, 2025 rename is still in force. The July 16, 2026 clarification is the current guidance.

### Gaps
- I did not find the exact Creator Insider video URL or the updated Help Center wording for the three July 2026 categories. I could not confirm whether the Help page text was expanded to match them; the 1311392 page as fetched still shows the July 2025 wording.
- I found no official YouTube enforcement counts for inauthentic-content demonetizations specifically.

## 2. Reused content vs inauthentic/mass-produced content, and how AI-voiced, AI-visual videos are judged

### Takeaway
"Reused content" is about using other people's material (clips, downloads, re-uploads) without meaningful transformation. "Inauthentic content" is about the channel's own output being templated or mass-produced, whatever its source. An AI voice plus AI visuals is not banned in itself. The test is whether each video has a distinct concept, narrative, commentary or educational value that comes from a human creator's perspective, and whether disclosure is applied where it is needed.

### Cited Findings
- Reused content allowed: "clips for a critical review", "Reaction videos where you comment on the original video", and "Edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Reused content not allowed: "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative", "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications", and "Songs modified to change the pitch or speed, but are otherwise identical to the original song." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Inauthentic content explicitly names "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates ... without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Halprin (July 2026): the problem is videos that are "very generic and don't really have a narrative arc." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- YouTube's AI-disclosure Help page says disclosure "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money", so using AI is not itself a penalty. — [YouTube Help: Disclosing altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Reported January 2026 enforcement targeted channels with synthetic narration, stock footage and unsustainably fast upload rates. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline) (secondary)
- The reported Google cluster-termination system flags coordinated networks by "synced upload schedules, templated formats, and common production infrastructure." — [Bottle Rocket Content](https://www.bottlerocketcontent.com/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-scale-revealed/) (secondary; paper not independently verified)
- Collateral damage has been reported: human-made faceless channels say they were penalized by the anti-slop measures. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- Industry summaries of what is safe: a human clearly steers the creative direction, each video offers distinct value, the content does not look formulaic, and realistic synthetic media is disclosed. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-ai-monetization/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- What reviewers likely look for, synthesized from the above: (1) whether the scripts are researched and specific or generic filler, (2) whether there is a narrative arc or point of view, (3) whether videos vary in structure and not only in topic keyword, (4) whether the visuals are purpose-made or interchangeable stock and AI B-roll, (5) the upload cadence relative to plausible human effort, and (6) whether the channel looks like part of a network (same templates or infrastructure across many channels).
- "Original value" in practice means: original research or reporting, a distinct opinion or analysis, a consistent creator voice or persona with real perspective (not a fake expert), real editing choices, and sourced facts.
- A faceless channel running several sister channels from one template and toolchain is at risk of the cluster-level termination described above.

### Gaps
- YouTube has published no exact percentage or quantitative test for "enough" original value. It remains a reviewer judgment.
- I found no official YouTube statement that AI voices alone are penalized. Claims in some blogs that "videos relying on AI voices" are inherently ineligible appear to be over-reading.

## 3. The altered or synthetic content disclosure label

### Takeaway
Creators must tick "Yes" under AI use / altered content in YouTube Studio when realistic content is AI-made or meaningfully altered: a real person saying or doing something they didn't, altered real events or places, realistic fabricated scenes, or AI-generated music that is the main focus. Animated or clearly unrealistic content and production help (scripts, thumbnails, captions) do not need it. Disclosing does not hurt reach or monetization. Repeatedly failing to disclose can lead to forced labels, removal, or YPP suspension. Since May 2026, YouTube auto-applies labels when its systems detect undeclared photorealistic AI, and shows labels more prominently. Likeness detection opened to all adult creators in May 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Disclosure is required when content "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do", "Alters footage of a real event or place", "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur", or "Creates music that's the main focus of the video." — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Not required: beauty or special-effects filters, colour adjustment, cloning your own voice for dubs, script or thumbnail generation, upscaling, captions. — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- How: in YouTube Studio, select "Yes" for the "AI use" setting in the upload flow's attributes/details section. — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Label placement: in the player for photorealistic content, and in the expanded description for non-photorealistic or animated AI content. — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- "Disclosing AI content won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- YouTube auto-labels content made with its own AI tools, content carrying C2PA metadata, and AI alterations it detects internally. Creators who consistently fail to disclose face "manual application of a label, or penalties from YouTube, including removal of content or suspension" from YPP. — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- **May 2026 change:** YouTube announced on about May 26, 2026 that labels would be more prominent and that it would add automatic detection. From May 27, 2026, YouTube applies the label itself when it detects heavy photorealistic AI the creator didn't declare. Placement is directly below the player on long-form and as an overlay on Shorts; lightly altered or animated content gets it in the description. — [Variety, May 2026](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/youtube-ai-video-labels-automatic-detection-1236758865/) (paywalled; details via [MiniMatters](https://minimatters.com/youtube-ai-content-labeling-update-in-may-2026/) and [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/youtube-ai-content-disclosure-rules-2026/) search summaries)
- **Likeness detection:** expanded to all creators aged 18+ (reported as May 17, 2026). It identifies possible synthetic uses of an enrolled creator's face, and they can request removal in YouTube Studio. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-policy-2026-likeness-detection-and-the-no-fakes-act-what-creators-need-to-know); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/media/youtube-launches-likeness-detection-to-help-creators-fight-ai-impersonation-1.500317044)
- Animated content is excluded from the realistic-content disclosure requirement, which critics say lets AI kids' animation go unlabelled. — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/youtube-ai-kids-videos-56389/) (via search summary)
- Same source reports that creators with EU audiences have a labelling duty from Aug 2, 2026 (EU AI Act transparency obligations). — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-policy-2026-likeness-detection-and-the-no-fakes-act-what-creators-need-to-know) (secondary)

### Inferences
- For a fully AI-visual channel with photorealistic scenes (historical reconstructions, "realistic" news-style visuals, realistic people), disclose by default. There is no monetization cost, and auto-detection now makes non-disclosure both detectable and penalizable. Stylized or animated output technically does not require it, but ticking it is low-risk.
- The SynthID watermarks that Google tools (Veo, Imagen) embed, and C2PA metadata from other tools, are the likely signals behind auto-labelling. Expect AI made with those tools to be labelled anyway.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Variety's article (paywall), so the exact May 2026 wording comes from secondary summaries.
- I found no confirmation of whether a creator can contest an auto-applied label.
- I found no YouTube primary source explicitly naming SynthID as a detection signal; it is inferred.

## 4. YPP eligibility thresholds and Shorts monetization (including AI Shorts)

### Takeaway
Current rules (in force until Jan 31, 2027): the full YPP (ad revenue) needs 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10M Shorts views in 90 days. The expanded tier, available in India, needs 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 watch hours or 3M Shorts views; it unlocks fan funding and Shopping only. **From Feb 1, 2027, new full-YPP applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 watch hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views in 90 days.** Shorts Creator Pool payouts will also require keeping 10M Shorts views per 90 days. Existing partners are not affected for entry. A Hindi AI channel starting now should plan around the 2027 thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- Current full YPP: "1,000 subscribers with 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months" or "1,000 subscribers with 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days". Also required: 2-step verification, a linked AdSense for YouTube account, advanced features enabled, no active Community Guidelines strikes, and residence in an eligible country. — [YouTube Help 72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)
- Shorts views do not count toward watch hours. Only public Shorts in the Shorts Feed count toward Shorts views; private, unlisted, deleted videos and image posts are excluded. — [YouTube Help 72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)
- Expanded YPP: 500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads in 90 days, and 3,000 watch hours in 12 months or 3M Shorts views in 90 days. It unlocks memberships, Super Chat and Super Stickers, Super Thanks, Jewels and gifts, and Shopping. **India is on the eligible list.** — [YouTube Help 13429240](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240)
- **Changes effective Feb 1, 2027 (announced Aug 10, 2026):** — [YouTube Help 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
  - The new entry threshold is 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days or 20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days.
  - Shorts Creator Pool: creators must keep "10M qualified Shorts views over the last 90 days" to earn that month. Missing it does not remove the creator from YPP.
  - Activity rule: a channel stays active with 1,000 watch hours in 365 days, or 1M Shorts views in 90 days, or 2 long-form videos or 5 Shorts uploaded every 90 days. Inactive channels get a 90-day window to recover.
  - "If you are already in YPP, your status is not impacted by this update."
  - Creators must accept the updated terms in Studio by Jan 31, 2027, or lose the related monetization features from Feb 1, 2027.
- The 500-subscriber fan-funding tier is unchanged in the 2027 overhaul. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/); [Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-updates-its-partner-program/)
- Re-uploaded Shorts reportedly lose reach because YouTube favours original clips. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips) (title only; not fetched)
- YouTube began testing (around March 2026) a mobile pop-up asking viewers to rate on a five-point scale whether a video feels like AI slop. — [Futurism / search summary](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/youtube-shutting-down-ai-slop-channels) (secondary)

### Inferences
- No separate AI-specific Shorts rule was found. AI Shorts fall under the same inauthentic, reused and disclosure policies. Cheap high-volume AI Shorts face double pressure: the 10M/90-day Creator Pool floor from 2027, plus "template" detection.
- A channel that has not joined YPP by Jan 31, 2027 faces double the watch-hour bar, so getting in before then on the current thresholds is a real strategic factor.

### Gaps
- I did not re-verify in this session the Shorts-specific monetization policy page listing ineligible Shorts (unedited movie or TV clips, re-uploads, compilations without original content). Those exclusions are known from earlier YouTube policy but are not cited here.
- I found no data on whether India-specific thresholds differ. None appear to.

## 5. Documented enforcement waves against AI slop channels

### Takeaway
Enforcement moved from demonetization (2025) to mass terminations (Dec 2025 to 2026). The key events: fake-trailer channels Screen Culture and KH Studio were demonetized in 2025 and terminated in Dec 2025. A Jan 2026 wave hit about 16 channels with about 35M subscribers and 4.7B views. A reported automated cluster system terminated about 130,000 channels in six months. Then came the July 2026 policy clarification.

### Cited Findings
- **Early 2025:** after a Deadline investigation, the AI fake-movie-trailer channels Screen Culture and KH Studio were demonetized. — [OutlierKit timeline](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/) (secondary, citing Deadline)
- **Aug 2025:** inquiries from The Guardian led to removals of AI slop channels, and YouTube began demonetizing high-volume AI channels. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/) (secondary)
- **Dec 2025:** Screen Culture and KH Studio, with 2M+ subscribers and 1B+ views between them, were terminated for AI fake trailers. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/); [Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/youtube-shutting-down-ai-slop-channels)
- **Jan 2026:** about 16 channels with 35M subscribers and 4.7B lifetime views were terminated or had their content wiped, earning an estimated ~$10M a year. Sources differ on the split: OutlierKit says 11 terminated and 6 wiped. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/); [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/youtube-removes-major-ai-slop-channels/)
  - Named examples: CuentosFacianantes (5.95M subscribers, AI Dragon Ball stories), Imperiodejesus (5.87M, AI Bible stories), Super Cat League (4.21M, AI animal content), and Three Minute Wisdom (1.7M, about 2B views, mostly wiped rather than terminated). — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/)
- **Google research paper** (reported as published Aug 2, 2026), "Scalable Detection of Adversarial Synthetic Slop and Coordinated Media Abuse: A LoRA-Enabled Multimodal Defense System". It describes a two-stage machine-learning "Scalable Cluster Termination System" that terminated 50,000 clusters, about 130,000 channels, in six months, with a claimed appeal-overturn rate under 1%. — [Bottle Rocket Content](https://www.bottlerocketcontent.com/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-scale-revealed/) (secondary; **paper not independently verified, treat with caution**)
- Kapwing's manual study of about 15,000 channels is referenced as evidence of how common AI slop is. — [Bottle Rocket Content](https://www.bottlerocketcontent.com/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-scale-revealed/) (no stats given)
- Reported collateral damage to human faceless creators. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- Industry coverage describes AI-animated kids' channels being demonetized. — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/youtube-demonitizes-popular-animated-channels/) (403 on fetch; details unverified)

### Inferences
- The genres hit hardest are fake trailers, AI religious and anime fan stories, AI animal "rescue" or cute content, and high-volume "wisdom" or motivational narration. These match the templated or emotionally manipulative categories. AI true-crime is widely discussed as at risk, but I found no specific dated termination of an AI true-crime channel. (The 2025 "True Crime Case Files" fake-case controversy is not confirmed in this research.)

### Gaps
- I could not verify the Google paper's existence or link. I could not fetch the original Deadline, Guardian or Kapwing primary reports. Channel-level figures come from secondary aggregators.

## 6. Copyright, music, imitating real people or voices, and kids content

### Takeaway
AI does not exempt a channel from copyright or reused-content rules. Real people can get realistic AI lookalike or soundalike content removed through YouTube's privacy process, and labels can request removal of AI clones of an artist's voice. Imitating a celebrity is a removal and strike risk, and it also requires disclosure. Kids' AI animation is exempt from the realistic-content label, but it is still subject to "made for kids" settings and the July 2026 "generic/template" and "off-putting" categories.

### Cited Findings
- "Songs modified to change the pitch or speed, but are otherwise identical to the original" count as reused content and are not monetizable. — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- AI music that is the main focus of a video must be disclosed. — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- Through YouTube's privacy complaint process, real people can request removal of realistic AI content simulating their face or voice. The uploader may get 48 hours to remove it before YouTube reviews it. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/you-can-now-make-takedown-requests-for-ai-generated-youtube-videos-that-mimic-your-likeness); [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/youtube-now-lets-you-remove-videos-with-your-ai-generated-likeness-or-voice/)
- Music partners can request removal of content that "mimics an artist's unique singing or rapping voice." This was announced in Nov 2023, initially for labels in YouTube's AI music experiments, with planned expansion. — [Digital Music News, Nov 2023](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2023/11/14/youtube-cracking-down-voice-cloning-synthetic-videos/) (older policy; likely still in force)
- Likeness detection is open to all creators 18+ (2026). Several US states now allow lawsuits over cloned voices, and the NO FAKES Act is under discussion. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-policy-2026-likeness-detection-and-the-no-fakes-act-what-creators-need-to-know)
- The July 2026 category 3 bars monetization of AI personas on finance, legal or health topics. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- AI-generated animation aimed at kids is not covered by the synthetic-content disclosure rule, because animated content is excluded. — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/youtube-ai-kids-videos-56389/) (search summary)
- March 2026 (aggregator): content showing minors in distress with shocking imagery lost ad eligibility. — [AIR Media-Tech timeline](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline)
- YouTube has framed kids and AI as part of its 2026 agenda. — [ChannelLife](https://channellife.news/story/youtube-sets-2026-agenda-on-creators-kids-ai-tools) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- For an AI kids' animation channel: content that is made for kids must be marked as such, which in YouTube's long-standing COPPA setup disables personalized ads, comments and some features, so revenue per view is usually lower. Templated nursery or animal AI animation is exactly the "generic/template" pattern the policy names. Kids' AI animation is a high-risk niche.
- Using a celebrity's AI face or voice (for example a Bollywood star or politician) is a significant risk on several fronts: privacy takedown, likeness-detection flags, misleading-content rules, disclosure duties, and in India the IT Rules impersonation timelines (see section 7).

### Gaps
- I did not fetch YouTube's current COPPA / "made for kids" Help page or its kids quality principles in this session. The statements about made-for-kids feature restrictions come from long-standing policy (FTC settlement, 2019–2020) and were not re-verified for 2026.
- I found no primary YouTube source on Content ID handling of AI-generated music that imitates copyrighted songs.

## 7. India-specific rules (Hindi audience) and a practical compliance checklist

### Takeaway
India's IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Amendment Rules 2026 took effect on Feb 20, 2026. They require large platforms like YouTube to get uploader declarations on whether content is synthetically generated information (SGI), to verify those declarations, and to label SGI prominently, with metadata where feasible. Takedown deadlines are very short. Indian creators should expect YouTube's AI declaration to be checked more strictly for Indian uploads, and impersonation or deepfake content to be removed fast.

### Cited Findings
- MeitY notified the amendment on Feb 10, 2026, effective Feb 20, 2026. — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=adb2714c-1188-43e3-b62b-eb6649727f6e); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn)
- SGI definition: audio, visual or audio-visual content "artificially or algorithmically created, generated, modified or altered ... in a manner that such information appears to be real, authentic, or true". AI-generated text is excluded. Also excluded: routine editing, good-faith document creation, and accessibility features. — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn)
- Labelling: SGI must be "clearly and prominently labelled", with visual labels for visuals and audio disclosures for audio, plus embedded metadata or identifiers where feasible. The draft's 10%-of-screen watermark proposal was dropped. — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn)
- Significant social media intermediaries (5M+ Indian users, which includes YouTube) must require uploaders to declare SGI and use automated tools to verify it. — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn)
- Takedown deadlines: 3 hours for court or government orders (was 36); 2 hours for high-risk content such as non-consensual imagery and impersonation (was 24); 36 hours for complaints about an individual; 7 days for general grievances. — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn)
- Platforms must inform users quarterly of the consequences of non-compliance (suspension, removal, legal penalties). — [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn)

### Practical compliance checklist for a faceless AI channel (synthesized from the sources above)
1. **Each video must be distinct.** Use a unique script with original research, analysis or narrative arc. Do not produce videos by find-and-replace, and vary structure, not only the topic. ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/))
2. **Keep a human creative point of view.** A consistent channel voice, opinions and commentary. Reusing intros and outros is fine.
3. **No AI "expert" personas on health, finance or law.** Narrating sourced research is fine; a synthetic "Dr." or "CA" avatar giving advice is not monetizable. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/))
4. **No manipulative or distressing formats.** No fake rescues, no rage-bait thumbnails without payoff, and no artificially stretched runtimes.
5. **Tick the AI-use disclosure** for any photorealistic AI visuals, realistic fake scenes, altered real events, or AI music as the main focus. It costs nothing in reach or revenue, and YouTube auto-labels anyway. For Indian uploads it is also the declaration the IT Rules 2026 require. ([YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn))
6. **No real-person imitation.** Do not use the AI face or voice of celebrities, politicians or artists, and do not use AI music that mimics a specific artist. ([MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/you-can-now-make-takedown-requests-for-ai-generated-youtube-videos-that-mimic-your-likeness))
7. **Use licensed or original assets only.** No unedited film or TV clips, no pitch-shifted songs, no re-uploads. Use licensed stock or YouTube Audio Library music. ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392))
8. **Keep a sustainable cadence.** Avoid upload volumes that signal automation, and do not run networks of near-identical sister channels on the same template and infrastructure. That is the cluster-termination pattern. ([Bottle Rocket](https://www.bottlerocketcontent.com/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-scale-revealed/))
9. **No fake trailers or fabricated "true stories"** presented as real.
10. **Kids' content:** if it is made for kids, set it as such and expect lower revenue. Avoid templated AI nursery or animal content.
11. **Thresholds:** aim to qualify by Jan 31, 2027 under 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 hours or 10M Shorts views. After that the bar is 8,000 hours or 20M Shorts views. Accept the new YPP terms by Jan 31, 2027. Keep meeting the 2027 activity rule (for example 2 long-form videos or 5 Shorts per 90 days). ([YouTube Help 12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en))
12. **Account hygiene:** 2-step verification, AdSense, advanced features, no active strikes. ([YouTube Help 72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851))

### Inferences
- A Hindi faceless channel using photorealistic AI visuals has two disclosure regimes, YouTube's policy and India's legal mandate. Both point the same way: always declare.
- Since the IT Rules' 2-hour deadline covers impersonation, deepfakes of Indian public figures could be removed almost immediately once flagged.

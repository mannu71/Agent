# Sleeve D1: Nifty last-half-hour intraday momentum. Evidence for tuning signal, threshold, stop, cost and gate (as of Oct 2026)

Scope: the published evidence behind each D1 rule (signal s = F(15:00)/prior close − 1, trade only if |s| ≥ 70th percentile of the prior 250 days, 0.75% catastrophic stop, exit 15:28, one lot of 65, cost 19 points base / 38 stress, switch-on gate of mean move ≥ 2× cost with t > 3 and ≥ 250 trades). Code read: `trading-agent/src/d1.cpp` and `include/ta/d1.hpp`. Configurable keys in `src/settings.cpp` are only `d1.pct_threshold`, `d1.stop_frac`, `d1.lot_size`, `d1.cost_points`, `d1.min_sleeve_equity` and `d1.risk.*`. `signal_time`, `exit_time`, `lookback_days`, `min_history` and `skip_days` are struct fields with no config key.

Access note: SSRN, ScienceDirect, Wiley, ResearchGate, DiVA, IDEAS/RePEc, Substack and the NSE site were blocked. Full texts were read from mirrors: the Baltussen et al. JFE published PDF (Notre Dame author page), the Gao et al. SSRN working paper (a public mirror), the Li-Sakkas-Urquhart accepted manuscript (Reading CentAUR), an arXiv paper, and a GitHub replication README.

## 1. Gao-Han-Li-Zhou (2018) and Baltussen-Da-Lammers-Martens (2021): signal definitions, best predictor, conditional strength, magnitudes, post-publication performance

### Takeaway
Baltussen's rest-of-day return (prior close to 30 minutes before the close) is the stronger and more robust predictor. It beat the Gao first-half-hour return on t-statistic, out-of-sample R² and timing-strategy Sharpe, so D1's Baltussen form is the right default. The edge is small: about 2.6–2.7 bps a day unconditionally, before costs. It is concentrated on negative-dealer-gamma, high-volatility and crisis days. US studies after the sample periods (2022–2026) find the unconditional effect flat.

### Cited Findings
**Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum" (JFE 129(2), 2018; SSRN 2440866)**
- Definitions: r1 is the first half-hour return, from the previous day's close to 10:00 ET (overnight plus first 30 minutes). r12 is the second-to-last half-hour (15:00–15:30). r13 is the last half-hour (15:30–16:00). Sample: SPY, Feb 1993–Dec 2013 — [Gao et al. SSRN working paper (mirror)](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf)
- r1 predicts r13 with an R² of 1.6%. r12 alone has an R² of 1.1%. Together the R² is 2.6%, roughly the sum, so the two are "independent and complementary". Predictability is significant out of sample (Campbell-Thompson OOS R²) — [Gao et al.](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf)
- Volatility terciles (sorted on first-half-hour volatility, joint r1 + r12 model): R² is **0.6% with r1 insignificant** on low-volatility days, 1.0% on medium days and **3.3% on high-volatility days**. Predictability also rises with first-half-hour volume (terciles formed year by year), on recession days and on some major macro-news days — [Gao et al.](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf)
- Crisis dependence: in Dec 2007–Jun 2009 the r1 slope is 13.6 with an R² of 4.1% (6.9% jointly). Excluding the crisis, the R² falls to 0.8% for r1 (1.1% jointly). The recursive r1 slope is "fairly stable" outside the crisis — [Gao et al.](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf)
- Timing strategy η(r1) (long the last half-hour if r1 > 0, short otherwise): **6.67% a year, standard deviation 6.19%, Sharpe 1.08**, against 6.04% and Sharpe 0.29 for buy-and-hold. Always Long in the last half-hour is −1.11% a year and insignificant — [Gao et al.](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf)
- Costs (Table 10, SPY, after decimalisation, entering at the 15:30 ask or bid and exiting at the 16:00 closing cross with no spread; commissions ignored): η(r1) falls by 2.47 percentage points to **4.46% a year**. Since 2005 it is 6.52% net against 7.96% gross. Using r1 and r12 together gives 4.30% net. The proportional spread stabilised around **1.2 bp after 2005** — [Gao et al.](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf)

**Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens, "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum" (JFE 142, 2021, 377–403; SSRN 3760365)**
- Definitions: r_ONFH = P(open+30)/P(close, t−1) − 1 (the Gao form). **r_ROD = P(close−30, t)/P(close, t−1) − 1** (rest of day). r_LH is the last 30 minutes. Hours are those of the underlying cash market, so the overnight return is measured from the previous cash-hours close. Over 60 futures, 1974–May 2020, tick data — [Baltussen et al. JFE PDF (author page)](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Pooled equity-index futures (Table 2): β_ONFH = 4.86 (t 6.52), R² 1.49%, **OOS R² −1.71%**. **β_ROD = 4.18 (t 7.29), R² 2.45%, OOS R² +2.88%**. Coefficients are ×100, so the last-30-minute return ≈ 0.042 × r_ROD. In the three-part split, the second-to-last half-hour carries the largest slope (β_SLH 9.08, t 4.82) — [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Horse race (Table 3): when r_ONFH and r_ROD are both included, r_ROD is "the strongest predictor for r_LH for equities, bonds, and commodities, leaving r_ONFH insignificant". On equal-sign days β_ROD is 4.26 with an R² of 3.13% — [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Subsamples (Table 5), equity: 1974–1999 β_ROD 5.96, R² 3.41%. **2000–2020 β_ROD 3.98 (t 6.65), R² 2.34%** — [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Timing strategies (Table 6, equally weighted portfolio of equity-index futures, no costs): η(r_ONFH) 4.21% a year, SR 1.07, success 0.55. η(r_ONFH, r_ROD), which trades only when the signs agree, 5.47%, **SR 1.60, success 0.61**. **η(r_ROD) 6.86%, vol 3.96%, SR 1.73, success 0.55**. Always Long 0.44%, SR 0.11 — [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Costs: "we do not consider transaction costs". The authors say the strategy "might not be exploitable to many investors after accounting for transaction costs". S&P 500 futures still give a positive net Sharpe at a cost of one tick — [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Dealer gamma (Table 7, S&P 500 options, 1996–May 2020). Net gamma exposure (NGE) assumes all calls are sold by investors to market makers and all puts are bought from them. **On NGE(t−1) < 0 days β_ROD = 6.63 (t 4.78), R² 3.58%. On NGE ≥ 0 days β_ROD = 0.82 (t 1.03), R² 0.05%, i.e. no momentum.** There were 2,930 negative-NGE and 3,158 positive-NGE days. The result holds with r_ONFH as the predictor — [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Leveraged ETFs (Table 9, equity futures, 2006–2020): the LETF share × r_ROD interaction is 1.79 (t 2.20). Markets with larger LETF shares show stronger momentum. Cheng & Madhavan estimate LETF rebalancing at 16.8% (50.2%) of market-on-close volume on a 1% (5%) market move — [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Last-half-hour price pressure **reverses over the next 1–3 days** in all four asset classes, so positions must be closed the same day — [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Asian coverage: Nikkei 225 futures (SGX, 1997–2020) and the Nikkei index are in the sample. **India/Nifty is not** — [Baltussen et al., Table 1 / appendix](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)

**Global follow-up: Li, Sakkas & Urquhart, "Intraday time series momentum: global evidence and links to market characteristics" (J. Financial Markets 57, 2022)**
- Sixteen developed markets (no India). The first half-hour predicts the last half-hour in 12 of 16, with significant OOS power in most. Pooled slope 3.71 in the financial crisis vs 2.09 outside it (R² 1.18% vs 0.63%). Recession vs expansion: slope 4.05 vs 2.52, R² 1.72% vs 0.81%. Low-liquidity markets give "almost doubled" strategy returns. Equally weighted global portfolio Sharpe 1.26–1.77 over 2005–2017 — [CentAUR accepted version](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/95566/1/Accepted-Version.pdf)

**Post-publication / recent evidence (US)**
- SPX, 1,085 sessions, 2022-04-14 to 2026-08-20, regressing the last 30 minutes on open-to-close-minus-30 (not from the prior close): slope **+0.006 ± 0.009 (t 0.6)**. By year: 2022 +0.009 (t 0.4), 2023 +0.002, 2024 −0.016, 2025 +0.016 (t 1.0), 2026 −0.012. "The unconditional effect is gone." With a trade-signed 0DTE dealer book read at 15:30, the slope rises by **+0.055 (t 3.1) on short-gamma closes**, but these are only ~15% of sessions, the effect is unstable in size, and "the public open-interest convention book sees none of it." Source is a data vendor's blog (commercial interest; not peer reviewed) — [FirmTape on DEV Community](https://dev.to/firmtape/intraday-momentum-is-dead-in-the-0dte-era-we-measured-it-on-1085-spx-sessions-43g0)
- Zarattini "Beat the Market" noise-area (an intraday-momentum variant) replicated on SPY and ES. Sharpe 1.11 vs 1.33 in the paper, **+2.6 bps a trade**. "Edge compressed since 2025: recent Sharpe ≈ 0 on both instruments." The fixed paper configuration had Sharpe 2.46 (2021), 1.70 (2022), 2.00 (2023), 1.51 (2024), **−0.27 (2025), −1.91 (2026 to July)**. Quarterly walk-forward reselection over 27 variants did *worse*: Sharpe 0.57 vs 0.92 fixed, with 14 switches in 19 quarters. The in-sample best of the 27 variants was the paper's original configuration — [giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy README and walk-forward report](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)
- Gamma-hedging mechanism extended to single stocks: intraday momentum is stronger when active option traders are net short gamma and outside hedgers' break-even ranges (Park & Zhao, Sept 2025 working paper) — [NFA paper](https://portal.northernfinanceassociation.org/viewp.php?n=2240183764)
- An independent 2026 arXiv falsification study of 14 OHLCV intraday-momentum signal families on MNQ futures (2021–2025, walk-forward) found none passed t ≥ 2 net with year stability. Eleven failed because the gross edge (0.07–1.5 points) was below about 2 points of friction. It did not test the last-half-hour signal specifically — [arXiv 2605.04004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04004)

### Inferences
- Unconditional magnitude: 6.67%/yr (Gao) and 6.86%/yr (Baltussen equity portfolio) are about **2.6–2.7 bps a day** at 252 days (my conversion). The 2000–2020 equity slope of ~0.040 means the expected last-30-minute move is about 4% of the move to 15:00.
- Keep the Baltussen r_ROD form as the default. The Gao form had negative pooled OOS R² in equity futures and loses the horse race. Testing it adds a trial with low prior odds.
- The edge is a hedging-flow effect that switches off when dealers are long gamma, and it has been flat in US data since 2022. The prior for a 2026 deployment should therefore be well below the in-sample papers.

### Gaps
- Gao et al.'s per-ETF tables and the exact news-day effect sizes were not extracted.
- No peer-reviewed post-2020 out-of-sample test of r_ROD was found. The 2022–2026 SPX result is from a vendor blog, and the original firmtape.com full report was not read.
- Exact Baltussen per-contract numbers for Nikkei (the closest Asian analogue) are in appendix tables that did not extract cleanly.

## 2. Indian evidence: Nifty/Bank Nifty intraday momentum, expiry days, option-dealer gamma, absence of leveraged ETFs, net-of-cost tests

### Takeaway
There is no peer-reviewed, net-of-cost test of last-half-hour momentum on Nifty futures. The only India-specific paper is an unrefereed August 2024 ResearchGate working paper, whose full text could not be read. India's settlement mechanics differ from the US in ways that matter for D1. The Nifty close and options final settlement are a 15:00–15:30 weighted average, not a closing auction. Weekly expiry moved to Tuesday from 1 Sept 2025, and only one weekly index expiry per exchange has existed since 20 Nov 2024.

### Cited Findings
- "Hedging Demand and Intraday Momentum within the Indian Stock Market" (Aug 2024, ResearchGate) adapts Baltussen-style regressions and NGE signals to Indian markets. It finds "strong intraday momentum towards the last half hour", says "certain elements ... translate well", and says Indian microstructure "necessitates tailored approaches". Unrefereed; full text blocked — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383567351_Hedging_Demand_and_Intraday_Momentum_within_the_Indian_Stock_Market)
- A search-engine summary (not verified against any document) attributed to Indian research an OOS R² of 2.96% for "the last 20 minutes of returns on NSE Nifty futures". Treat it as **unverified**; it probably comes from the ResearchGate paper above — [ResearchGate listing (probable source)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383567351_Hedging_Demand_and_Intraday_Momentum_within_the_Indian_Stock_Market)
- One public Indian backtest of "first 30 minutes predicts last 30 minutes" on Nifty 100 *stocks* (not futures; survivorship-biased constituents) captured about **1.5 bps a trade against ~20 bps costs** — [md0n-cmd/indian-equity-backtests](https://github.com/md0n-cmd/indian-equity-backtests) (as recorded in the earlier research notes for this project)
- NSE settlement mechanics: the futures daily settlement price is the weighted average price over the last 30 minutes. Nifty/Bank Nifty options final settlement uses the index VWAP over **15:00–15:30**. If too few trades occur, NSCCL uses a theoretical price — [tradingqna (Zerodha) thread](https://tradingqna.com/t/what-is-the-difference-between-daily-and-final-settlement-in-derivatives/4925); [marketcalls on VWAP settlement](https://www.marketcalls.in/amibroker/how-to-estimate-the-vwap-based-settlement-close-like-a-pro-using-amibroker.html); [NSE settlement mechanism page (search extract)](https://nseindia.com/products/content/derivatives/irf/settlement_mechanism.htm)
- Expiry calendar: under SEBI's October 2024 circular (effective 20 Nov 2024), each exchange may offer weekly expiry on only one benchmark index. From **1 Sept 2025 Nifty weekly expiry moved from Thursday to Tuesday**, and Sensex (BSE) moved to Thursday. Bank Nifty, FinNifty and similar weeklies ended with the Nov 2024 rule — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/nse-bids-adieu-to-thursday-expiry-as-dates-swap-come-into-effect-explained-125082800635_1.html); [Angel One](https://oga-prod.angelone.in/news/market-updates/why-nifty-expiry-changed-to-tuesday); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/nse-receives-sebi-nod-for-tuesday-expiry-bse-thursday-480736-2025-06-17)
- Asian analogues: titles exist on SSRN for "Delta-Hedging Demand and Intraday Momentum: Evidence from China" (Yuan & Li, Physica A, Aug 2022), "Market Intraday Momentum in Japan: Evidence from the Nikkei Stock Index" (Jin, 2024) and "Cross-Market Intraday Time-Series Momentum" (Xu, Li, Singh, Li, 2024). No abstracts or numbers were retrievable — [Crossref 10.1016/j.physa.2022.127508](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2022.127508); [Crossref 10.2139/ssrn.4816793](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4816793); [Crossref 10.2139/ssrn.4765613](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4765613)

### Inferences
- **Closing-price mechanics.** Because the Nifty close and option settlement are a 15:00–15:30 average, hedgers who target the settlement price spread their flow across the whole window D1 holds (15:00–15:28). This supports the 15:00 entry. It also means D1 should **not** use the official daily settlement price as "prior close", because that is a VWAP. The code correctly uses the last traded bar's close.
- **Expiry days.** These are where Indian dealer gamma is most concentrated and the 15:00–15:30 settlement VWAP is being "traded at". Pinning (dealers long gamma) and squeezes (dealers short gamma) push in opposite directions, so the sign of the expiry-day effect is an empirical question. Test it as an explicit variant, not a default skip. A historical `skip_days` list must use the actual expiry weekday of each period: Thursday until Aug 2025, Tuesday from Sept 2025, plus pre-Nov-2024 Bank Nifty/FinNifty weeklies if testing an "any index expiry" skip.
- **Gamma sign convention.** Baltussen's NGE assumes end users are long puts and short calls. In India, retail is widely reported to be a heavy *buyer* of both calls and puts, so the dealer/prop book may be short gamma on both sides. The US "OI convention" also failed in the 0DTE era (FirmTape). An NSE open-interest NGE proxy built from bhavcopy is therefore a speculative feature that needs its own pre-registered test.
- **Leveraged ETFs.** These are one of Baltussen's two channels (interaction t 2.20), and India has no domestic leveraged-ETF rebalancing flow (not verified; see Gaps). On that channel alone, the Indian β_ROD would be expected to be *smaller* than the US/global estimate, not larger.
- **Contract roll (code-level risk).** `d1_signals` divides the 15:00 price of the current bar series by the prior day's last close. On the first day after a monthly roll in a continuous near-month series, those two prices come from different contracts. The basis difference, about one month of carry (very roughly 0.3–0.6% of price; my estimate), then enters s as a spurious positive "return" that is about as large as the 70th-percentile threshold. Compute s from the same contract (or from the spot index plus the futures basis) on roll days.

### Gaps
- The full text of the Indian working paper (sample, Nifty vs Bank Nifty, slopes, NGE results, any cost analysis) could not be read. ResearchGate is blocked and the paper is not indexed in Crossref, CORE or scholar.archive.
- No study of last-half-hour momentum on Indian expiry vs non-expiry days was found.
- No source was found confirming that SEBI prohibits leveraged ETFs in India. This is widely believed but unverified here.
- No Bank Nifty-specific last-half-hour evidence was found.

## 3. Replications and critiques: post-2020 decay, Zarattini noise-area losses, volatility-regime dependence, and whether threshold, volatility conditioning or expiry skips improve OOS results

### Takeaway
Every source agrees the effect is regime-dependent: weak or absent on low-volatility and dealer-long-gamma days, strongest in crises. Recent US data show the unconditional effect flat (2022–2026) and the noise-area variant losing in 2025–26. No paper tests a rolling percentile threshold on |s| out of sample. The only walk-forward parameter-reselection test found (noise-area) *destroyed* value relative to keeping the published parameters fixed.

### Cited Findings
- Volatility conditioning: Gao R² is 0.6% (low first-half-hour volatility, r1 insignificant) vs 3.3% (high). The crisis R² is 4.1% vs 0.8% outside it — [Gao et al.](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf)
- Li-Sakkas-Urquhart: the slope is larger in crises in 12 of 16 markets and larger in recessions in 12 of 16 — [CentAUR](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/95566/1/Accepted-Version.pdf)
- Gamma conditioning: the β_ROD gap between negative and non-negative NGE days is 6.63 vs 0.82 — [Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf). Since 2022, only trade-signed 0DTE dealer gamma (not the OI convention) shows conditional momentum (+0.055, t 3.1, ~15% of days) — [FirmTape](https://dev.to/firmtape/intraday-momentum-is-dead-in-the-0dte-era-we-measured-it-on-1085-spx-sessions-43g0)
- Sign agreement (a filter that does not depend on |s|): trading only when r_ONFH and r_ROD agree raised the success rate to 0.61 and gave SR 1.60 vs 1.73 for r_ROD alone, with a lower average return (5.47% vs 6.86%) — [Baltussen et al., Table 6](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)
- Noise-area replication: Sharpe 2025 −0.27 and 2026 −1.91. In the replicator's report, 2022 was +25.8% with SPY at −19.5%, a "long volatility" profile. Walk-forward reselection gave Sharpe 0.57 vs 0.92 fixed. Maróy's (2025) optimised Sharpe > 3 was not reproduced under a frozen protocol — [giovannibrusco replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)
- Publication decay in general: predictor returns fall after the original sample ends (the search summary quotes 26% between sample end and publication across 97 predictors) — [arXiv 2209.13623 "Publication Bias in Asset Pricing Research"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13623) (figure from a search extract, not verified in the full text)

### Inferences
- Under the linear model r_LH ≈ β·s, the expected gross move in the signal direction for days above a |s| percentile is β·E[|s| | |s| ≥ q]. For normally distributed s, E[|s| | top 30%] ≈ 1.55σ_s and E[|s| | top 10%] ≈ 2.06σ_s. My worked numbers at Nifty 25,000, with σ_s (std of the close→15:00 move) assumed at 0.7/0.9/1.2%:
  - β = 0.040–0.042 (global equity, 2000–2020), 70th pct: **11–20 points**; 90th pct: 14–26 points.
  - β = 0.066 (US negative-gamma days only), 70th pct: 18–31 points; 90th pct: 24–41 points.
  - The D1 gate needs ≥ 38 points (2 × 19). With global-average β it fails at any percentile except in a very high-volatility year. Even the US conditional-gamma β clears 38 points only at the 90th percentile with σ_s ≈ 1.2%. **The gate is reachable only if India's β is roughly 2.5–3× the global equity estimate.** This is a model-based inference; fat tails would raise the conditional |s| somewhat.
- A rolling percentile threshold is mainly a disguised volatility filter: big |s| happens on high-volatility days, which is where the literature finds predictability. A cost-aware form is better grounded. Trade only if β̂·|s|·F ≥ k × cost, with β̂ estimated walk-forward on Indian data. This makes the threshold explicit in rupees and adjusts automatically as the Nifty level and volatility change.
- Re-optimising the percentile through walk-forward is likely to hurt, as the noise-area experiment showed. Pre-register a small fixed set (e.g. 0.70 and 0.90) and count each as a trial.
- Given the flat US 2022–2026 result, any Indian in-sample success concentrated before 2022 should be discounted. Report 2022–2026 separately and require it to pass on its own.

### Gaps
- No study evaluates a percentile-of-|s| threshold, an India VIX filter or an expiry-day skip out of sample for last-half-hour momentum in any market.
- No post-2020 evidence for Asian or Indian index futures was found.
- The volatility-tercile magnitudes are in R² terms, and conditional bps per trade were not reported in the sources read.

## 4. Realistic cost of trading one Nifty futures lot (65 units) after the April 2026 STT change

### Takeaway
Statutory charges plus ₹20-a-leg brokerage come to about **0.059–0.060% of notional, about 15 points at Nifty 25,000**. Adding 1–2 points of slippage a leg gives **~17–19 points base**, so the current 19-point base is reasonable at today's index level. Charges scale with price, so a fixed point cost misstates costs over a 2017–2026 backtest in which Nifty ranged from about 9,000 to 26,000.

### Cited Findings
- Zerodha F&O-futures schedule (read Oct 2026): brokerage 0.03% or ₹20 per executed order, whichever is lower. **STT 0.05% on the sell side.** NSE transaction charge **0.00183%**. SEBI fee ₹10 per crore. Stamp duty **0.002% (₹200 per crore) on the buy side**. GST 18% on brokerage + SEBI fee + transaction charges — [Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges)
- STT on futures sales rose from 0.02% to 0.05% from 1 April 2026 (Budget 2026) — [ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/ilearn/futures-and-options/articles/stt-changes-in-budget-2026-what-f-o-traders-should-know); [Groww](https://groww.in/blog/what-is-stt). Conflict: one search summary gave futures STT as 0.01%, which is inconsistent with the broker schedule and the Budget-2026 sources and is disregarded — [search extract citing Navia](https://navia.co.in/blog/zero-brokerage-on-fo-charges-you-still-pay/)
- US comparison: Gao's measured SPY half-spread cost cut η(r1) by about 1–2.5 percentage points a year, with a spread of about 1.2 bp after 2005. The noise-area replication on ES cost about 0.4 bps per round trip — [Gao et al.](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf); [giovannibrusco](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)

### Inferences
- My arithmetic for one lot (65 × F), round trip with ₹20 × 2 brokerage:
  - At F = 22,000: STT ₹715, transaction ₹52, stamp ₹29, SEBI ₹3, brokerage ₹40, GST ₹17. Total **₹856 = 13.2 points = 5.99 bps**.
  - At F = 25,000: total **₹966 = 14.9 points = 5.95 bps**, of which STT is ₹812 (84%).
  - At F = 28,000: **₹1,076 = 16.6 points**.
  - Add slippage. The tick size was not verified. At 15:00 and 15:28 one lot is small relative to near-month depth, so assume 1–2 points a leg base and 4–5 points a leg stress (my assumptions; no Indian futures spread study was read). Base ≈ **17–19 points (≈7–7.5 bps)**; stress (2× slippage plus a margin) ≈ 25–38 points.
- Indian round-trip cost is about **6× the US half-spread cost** and roughly **2.5–3× the unconditional daily edge** in the US/global papers. The edge has to come almost entirely from conditioning.
- Because STT is a proportional tax, the backtest should model cost as a fraction of price: about 0.0006 × F plus a slippage term in points. A fixed 19 points overstates costs early in the sample (19 points at Nifty 10,000 ≈ 19 bps vs ≈ 7.6 bps at 25,000). That biases the early-period result toward failing and makes the 2×-cost gate harder to pass in early years than later ones.

### Gaps
- No independent measurement of Nifty near-month bid-ask spread or impact at 15:00 and 15:28 was found. The NSE impact-cost statistics site was blocked.
- The NSE transaction charge (0.00183%) comes from a broker page, not the NSE circular, and may be revised.
- Exchange IPFT/clearing charges, if any are passed on, are not itemised in the source read.

## 5. Rule-by-rule recommendations for D1 (config key or code change, with trial counting)

### Takeaway
Keep the Baltussen signal, 15:00 entry, 15:28 exit, one lot, 0.75% catastrophic stop and the 2×-cost gate. Replace the fixed-point cost with a proportional cost. Fix roll-day signal contamination. Add at most three pre-registered conditioning variants: expiry skip, a cost-aware or volatility threshold, and NSE-OI gamma. Count each as a trial. On the published slopes, the sleeve is more likely than not to stay at zero capital.

### Cited Findings
- The rule changes below rest on the findings cited in sections 1–4: r_ROD dominance and OOS R² ([Baltussen et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)), volatility/crisis dependence ([Gao et al.](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf); [Li et al.](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/95566/1/Accepted-Version.pdf)), post-2022 US flatness ([FirmTape](https://dev.to/firmtape/intraday-momentum-is-dead-in-the-0dte-era-we-measured-it-on-1085-spx-sessions-43g0)), walk-forward reselection harm ([giovannibrusco](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)), Indian costs ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/charges)) and the expiry calendar ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/nse-bids-adieu-to-thursday-expiry-as-dates-swap-come-into-effect-explained-125082800635_1.html)).

# Code the Flow, Not the Folklore

Nearly every "smart money" concept taught on YouTube can be written as an exact rule. Almost none of them, once written down, beats random entries after costs. The largest independent test codified **44 Smart Money Concepts (SMC) detectors and ran them over 496 S&P 500 stocks from 2010 to 2026. After multiple-testing correction, none beat a matched random entry at any horizon** ([SSRN 7483658](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7483658)). A separate set of 648 daily backtests of order blocks, fair value gaps, liquidity sweeps and optimal trade entry (OTE) on SPY, QQQ, DIA and IWM found **zero that beat buy-and-hold, even with no costs** ([StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives)). A 2026 G10 FX study, reported second-hand, found that swept liquidity predicts *continuation*, the opposite of the reversal ICT teaches ([ThorTradeCopier](https://thortradecopier.com/blog/does-ict-smart-money-concepts-work)). Wyckoff, Volume Spread Analysis (VSA), supply/demand zones and Al Brooks setups have no independent after-cost test at all, so they are untested rather than disproven. Order-flow imbalance explains the bar in which it occurs, but in crypto perpetuals its forecasting power is about one-tenth of a single taker fee ([arXiv 2607.09426](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)).

The edges with real evidence are the ones where institutional flow is *mechanical*. Last-half-hour intraday momentum is peer-reviewed, appears in more than 60 futures markets, and is driven by dealer hedging. The SPY "noise area" breakout with a VWAP trailing stop replicated through 2024 but ran flat to negative in 2025–26. The third is time-series trend. The academic case for "liquidity" itself is solid: stop orders cluster just beyond round numbers, and triggering them accelerates moves ([NY Fed SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). That makes "sweep, then continuation" the one SMC hypothesis worth a serious test.

India's footprints (delivery %, bulk and block deals, FII/DII flows, open-interest build-ups) are published only after the close. Their evidence is thin and never cost-adjusted, so they must be tested as next-day swing signals against a delivery round-trip cost of about 0.5%. The agent should spend its first test budget on three things: a crypto transplant of the noise-area/VWAP strategy, last-half-hour momentum on SPY and Nifty futures, and a pre-registered sweep-direction test on BTC/ETH/SOL. The full ICT stack gets one frozen, documented run, not a parameter search.

## Forty-four smart-money detectors could not beat random entries

### What ICT and SMC actually claim, and what code exists

Michael J. Huddleston's ICT method and the wider SMC vocabulary describe price as an algorithm. In this picture, price runs resting stops ("liquidity") above old highs and below old lows, leaves imbalances ("fair value gaps") when it moves fast, and returns to the last opposing candle before a move ("order block") to fill institutional orders. No written specification from ICT exists. His definitions live in YouTube lectures and paid mentorships, so every codeable version comes from secondary glossaries or from programmers' choices. The most complete open implementation is the `smartmoneyconcepts` Python package. It covers swings, BOS/CHoCH, FVGs, order blocks, liquidity, sessions and retracements, but **it has lookahead built in** ([smc.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/master/smartmoneyconcepts/smc.py)). Its swing detector uses a centred window, so a swing at bar *i* is only known *swing_length* bars later. Its liquidity tolerance is 1% of the *whole* dataframe's high-low range, which includes future bars. Its session times are hard-coded in UTC with no daylight-saving adjustment, while ICT anchors to New York time. A paper-trading agent that imports the package unmodified will backtest on information it could not have had.

The sympathetic codifiers concede the core problem. LuxAlgo describes ICT's 2022 model as "discretionary at every joint: pool selection, displacement energy, and gap identification vary by trader", with "no published edge proof" ([LuxAlgo Model 2022](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/model-2022.md)). Kill-zone boundaries themselves "differ by 30 to 90 minutes across ICT sources" ([LuxAlgo Killzones](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/killzones.md)). Each discretionary joint becomes a free parameter, and free parameters are where false discoveries come from.

### Three independent tests point the same way

The Tangirala working paper is the most important single piece of evidence, and it carries a lesson for the agent's own design. It translated two widely used open-source Pine scripts into 44 formal detectors and simulated 17.6 million trades across 8 holding horizons. **Against a zero-return null, 38 of 44 detectors looked significant. Against a matched random-entry null, with corrections across 352 concept-horizon hypotheses, none did.** The largest point estimates, 11–12 basis points over 10 days, belonged to the two liquidity-sweep detectors, with p ≥ 0.23. For 10 concepts the data rule out, at 95% confidence, an edge large enough to cover even an 11 bp round trip ([SSRN 7483658](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7483658)). The gap between those two nulls is the whole story: SMC events look profitable because stocks drift upward, and the events add nothing to that drift. Two caveats apply. The paper is an unreviewed SSRN working paper, and its full text could not be read here, so the figures come from its abstract.

StatOasis ran the four headline concepts on daily bars of four US index ETFs back to the 1990s, with zero commission and slippage. The best SPY result, an order-block variant at $110,040, finished far behind buy-and-hold at $551,738, though ahead of the random-entry mean of $46,270. Of 32 forward-edge t-statistics, **exactly one crossed 2** (OTE on DIA at 10 days, t = +2.07), which is what chance alone would produce ([StatOasis](https://statoasis.com/overfit/research/ict-backtest-what-survives)). The author makes the standard defence for ICT himself: daily tests "prove nothing" about a discretionary trader on a 5-minute chart at 9:47am. For an algorithmic agent that defence does not apply, because whatever the agent trades is code and can be tested.

The FX evidence turns the reversal story upside down. Mahadzva's 2026 SSRN study of EURUSD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD and USDJPY intraday is reported only via a secondary blog, and a direct title search failed. It found that three independently built sweep rules agreed in **65 of 66 fold selections that a swept level predicts continuation**. Its best survivor was a *faded* EURUSD 1-hour CHoCH strategy: +19.3% on 60 holdout trades, profit factor 1.50, p = 0.019. The abstract states: "We find no evidence that ICT/SMC concepts work as literally taught" ([ThorTradeCopier](https://thortradecopier.com/blog/does-ict-smart-money-concepts-work)). Sixty holdout trades is a thin base, so this is a hypothesis to test, not a result to trade.

The most popular claim, that "FVGs get filled", fails a base-rate check. In a simulated random walk of one million bars, **83.4% of bullish FVGs were revisited within 20 bars, against 83.5% of random levels at the same distance** ([ThorTradeCopier](https://thortradecopier.com/blog/does-ict-smart-money-concepts-work)). Any "level gets touched" statistic, whether for FVGs, naked POCs or order blocks, is meaningless unless it beats random levels. Promotional figures fail the same way: a preprint claiming a 78% win rate and +265R on 473 FVG trades reports no out-of-sample test, random baseline or costs.

### Stop clustering is real, and it argues for continuation

ICT's liquidity story has a real core, and it comes from Federal Reserve research, not from YouTube. Carol Osler studied 9,655 actual orders worth more than $55 billion at a major dealing bank in 1999–2000 and found that **14.3% of executed buy-stops sat just above round numbers (rates ending 01–10), against 6.9% just below**. Take-profit orders clustered *at* the round number. On one-minute FX quotes, crossing a round number produced larger and longer-lasting moves than crossing an arbitrary level: 0.061% vs 0.054% over 15 minutes, with round numbers higher in 51 of 58 ten-day intervals. The post-crossing movement stayed significant for at least two hours, while reversals at take-profit clusters faded within 30 minutes ([NY Fed SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). Osler names this effect "price cascades", and it is the mechanism behind "running the stops". The mechanism predicts that when price takes out a stop cluster the move tends to *continue*. That fits the FX continuation finding and the small positive long-side sweep estimates in US stocks. It does not fit ICT's "sweep then reverse". None of this has been tested on crypto or NSE, so the agent's own results would be new evidence.

## Wyckoff, VSA, zones and Brooks are untested, not disproven

### A century-old vocabulary with no canonical thresholds

SMC is often described as Wyckoff renamed. The Wyckoff schematic has more structure than ICT's, but the same problem: **Wyckoff Analytics publishes no quantitative thresholds for event size, phase length or volume.** The method "relies on pattern recognition and relative comparisons" ([Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/)). The closest thing to an official algorithm is a 2025 Wyckoff Analytics article on spring detection. It gives these rules: the spring bar must not close at its low; volume should rise on the penetration, but the bar must close back above support; consecutive lows more than 10% apart are unacceptable; and the Pine baseline lookback is 15 bars, with 20–25 as alternatives. It reports no backtest, volume threshold, entry, stop or target ([Wyckoff Analytics springs](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/identifying-wyckoff-springs-with-algorithmic-trading-strategies/)).

Tom Williams' VSA reads each bar as effort (volume) against result (spread and close location). Its one exact published rule is the no-demand bar: an up bar with a narrow spread and volume below *each* of the previous two bars. VSA's own sources treat it as evidence that needs context, not as a trigger ([LuxAlgo no-demand bars](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/no-demand-no-supply-bars/)). Supply and demand zones (rally-base-rally, drop-base-rally and their mirrors) have been codified in TradingView scripts. One author admits that the core doctrines, that fresh zones beat tested ones and heavy-volume bases beat quiet ones, are "measured almost nowhere" ([TradingView Zone Forge](https://br.tradingview.com/scripts/trendanalysis/page-8/)). Al Brooks' bar counting is the most precisely defined of the group. An H1 is a bar whose high exceeds the prior bar's high in a bull flag. An H2 is the next such bar after at least one more lower-high bar ([Brooks Trading Course forum](https://www.brookstradingcourse.com/support-forum/01-terminology/h1-h2-l1-l2-etc/paged/2/)). An open-source indicator adds an EMA trend filter and requires the signal bar to close in its top 30% ([TradingView Al Brooks Second Entry](https://www.tradingview.com/script/YUixSlPL-Al-Brooks-Second-Entry/)).

The evidence search found **no peer-reviewed or independent systematic backtest of Wyckoff schematics, VSA bar signs, rally-base-rally zones or Brooks H2 entries showing profit after costs**. Forum attempts to backtest Wyckoff and zones published no results ([Aussie Stock Forums](https://www.aussiestockforums.com/threads/backtesting-wyckoff-method.33512/); [Optuma forum](https://forum.optuma.com/t/supply-and-demand-zones/3392)). Machine-learning Wyckoff papers report *detection* accuracy, not trading profit ([JISEM 2024](https://www.jisem-journal.com/download/60_Wyckoff%20Theory.pdf)). These methods therefore sit at grade C. A grade of C means there is no evidence either way, which is different from negative evidence.

### The adjacent literature says levels matter and rule-mining fails

Three academic findings bracket these methods. First, Osler's earlier study found that FX rates **bounced off published support and resistance levels 60.8% of the time, against 56.2% for arbitrary levels**. All 16 firm-currency pairs beat random levels, and the effect lasted five business days ([Osler 2000](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). That shows levels have predictive content. It is not a profitability test, and a 4.6-point bounce edge over 15 minutes is small against spreads. Second, Lo, Mamaysky and Wang showed that chart patterns can be detected algorithmically (smooth the price, find extrema, match geometry) and that "several technical indicators do provide incremental information" ([MIT](https://web.mit.edu/Alo/www/Papers/techanal.html)). That is the template for coding Wyckoff ranges or zone bases. Third, the rule-mining literature is unforgiving. Sullivan, Timmermann and White applied White's Reality Check to Brock et al.'s trading-range breakout and moving-average rules. Out of sample from 1987 to 1996, the best rule earned an insignificant 8.63% a year before costs ([LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119144)). Bajgrowicz and Scaillet found that on DJIA data from 1897 to 2011, investors "would never have been able to select ex ante the future best-performing rules", and that even in-sample profits are "completely offset by the introduction of low transaction costs" ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p473-491.html)). Each of the modules above has 3–8 free parameters; three values each gives hundreds of configurations per market per timeframe. That is exactly the setting in which these papers show apparent edges disappearing.

"Trade higher lows in an uptrend" does have an A-grade relative, though the evidence belongs to the trend, not to the higher low. Time-series momentum was positive in every asset class across 58 futures from 1985 to 2009 ([SSRN 2089463](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463)). Trend following has been profitable since 1880 ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing)). Crypto shows strong time-series momentum at weekly horizons ([NBER w24877](https://nber.org/papers/w24877)). The pullback-timing overlay has weaker and recently negative evidence. A Connors RSI(2) dip-buy above the 200-day average won 71% of trades on the S&P 500 from 2016 to 2026 but returned **+18.1% in total against +255.4% for buy-and-hold** ([Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/connors-rsi-2-sp-500)). A high win rate with low time in the market loses to simply staying invested. The test for the agent is therefore whether a higher-low or H2 trigger beats a *random entry inside the same trend*, not whether it makes money.

## Order flow explains the current bar, not the next one

### The academic horizon ladder

Order-flow teachers (Michael Valtos's Orderflows, Fabio Valentini's absorption-accumulation-aggression model, Axia Futures, Trader Dale's volume clusters) sell the idea that seeing aggressive buyers and sellers reveals institutional intent. The academic literature agrees that flow *moves* price. Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov found a linear relation between order-flow imbalance and price change, with the slope inversely proportional to market depth ([arXiv 1011.6402](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402)). On BitMEX perpetuals, signed taker volume explains price change better than book imbalance ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/digfin/v1y2019i1d10.1007_s42521-019-00007-w.html)). But these are *contemporaneous* relations, and forecasting power decays fast. In NYSE data, lagged imbalances predicted returns for **30 minutes in 1996, 10 minutes in 1999 and 5 minutes in 2002** ([CFA Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/en/research/cfa-digest/2005/11/evidence-on-the-speed-of-convergence-to-market-efficiency-digest-summary)). Chordia and Subrahmanyam's daily imbalance strategy was profitable on paper, but "transaction costs will mitigate any profits" ([UPenn PDF](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/stock-misbalance.pdf)).

Crypto results are the most relevant to the agent, and they are discouraging at retail costs. A 2026 study of Binance USDT-M perps for BTC, ETH, SOL and three others (2021–2024, 10-second bars) found that one-minute imbalance has "little predictive association" with short-horizon returns. Quarter-hour opening imbalance does predict 4–12-hour returns, but the sign-weighted forecast is about **0.50 bp per event, "one tenth of a single standard-tier taker fee"**, and the authors conclude it is not profitable after costs ([arXiv 2607.09426](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)). A 1-second-data machine-learning study predicting 3-second returns made money after fees only on small coins (ETC, ENJ, ROSE), not on BTC ([arXiv 2602.00776](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00776v1)). A Journal of Financial Markets paper reportedly finds crypto order-flow predictability robust to high costs, but its horizon and construction could not be verified ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1386418126000029)). VPIN, the "flow toxicity" measure, is contested as a predictor ([Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/the_trouble_with_vpin)). For BTC and ETH it informs volatility, hedging and market making, not direction ([Cornell PDF](https://stoye.economics.cornell.edu/docs/Easley_ssrn-4814346.pdf)).

On Delta Exchange India, taker fees are 0.05% per side, so a round trip costs 10 bp before any GST on fees. Earlier project work put it at about 12 bp with 18% GST; that was not re-verified this round. Even a maker-maker round trip, at 4 bp, exceeds every measured signal size. **Order flow therefore belongs in the agent as a conditioning filter, not as a trigger.** Examples are not fading strong one-sided aggression, or sizing down when toxicity is high.

### Profile and auction statistics are base rates, not edges

Jim Dalton's Market Profile and volume-profile teaching supply the most quoted statistics in the field, and the independent numbers fall short of the names. The "80% rule" is: open outside yesterday's value area, then two consecutive 30-minute periods back inside, targeting the far edge. It succeeded "only 60% of the time" on E-mini S&P, and the tester said it "should probably be called the 60% rule" ([mypivots](https://www.mypivots.com/dictionary/definition/25/80-rule)). Another source claims 65–70% with no method described ([MetroTrade](https://www.metrotrade.com/what-is-the-80-rule-in-futures-trading/)). Initial-balance (IB) breakouts happen on **97.8% of ES days and 96.2% of NQ days**, so "the IB will break" carries no information. A full 100% extension by the close occurs on only 13–21% of days, and the study computed no P&L ([tradingstats.net](https://tradingstats.net/initial-balance-breakout-statistics/)). The claim that "80% of naked POCs are revisited within 10 sessions" has no random-level comparison ([LuxAlgo Naked POC](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/naked-poc/)). The FVG random-walk result shows why that omission is fatal.

## Real institutional edges are mechanical, thin and decaying

### VWAP, opening range and intraday momentum have the only strong evidence

VWAP is an institutional fact, not folklore. Berkowitz, Logue and Noser's 1988 proposal made daily VWAP the standard execution benchmark, and participation algorithms slice orders along the expected volume curve ([Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)). Dealers hedging short-gamma options positions, and leveraged ETFs rebalancing near the close, add predictable same-direction flow late in the day. Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens tied the "last half-hour" effect to exactly this hedging demand across **more than 60 futures from 1974 to 2020**. Their best signal is the return from the prior close to 30 minutes before today's close, and it predicts the final 30 minutes. A timing strategy on a 1/N equity-futures portfolio earned 6.86% a year at a **Sharpe of 1.73**, against 0.11 for always-long in the same window. It kept a positive Sharpe after one tick of cost in S&P futures ([JFE PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). Gao, Han, Li and Zhou's original SPY version (1993–2013) made 6.67% a year at Sharpe 1.08. Entering at the 15:30 bid or ask and exiting at the spread-free closing auction cut that to **4.46% a year after costs** ([paper PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)). This is the closest thing to grade A in the whole field, and nothing about it is "smart money" in the YouTube sense. It is the price footprint of rule-bound hedgers.

The Concretum papers by Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon formalise the opening-range and VWAP ideas retail teachers sell, but their independent replications tell a sober story. Note that co-author Andrew Aziz runs the Bear Bull Traders education business ([Bear Bull Traders](https://bearbulltraders.com/category/andrews-newsletter/page/3/)).

Three lessons from the table shape the agent. First, **per-trade edges are a few basis points (index ETFs) or about 0.1R (stocks), so execution cost decides viability**. The QQQ ORB breaks even at about 2.2¢ a share of slippage, and its profits cluster in volatile years such as 2022. Second, **frozen published parameters beat re-optimisation**. A 27-variant grid chose the paper's own configuration, and quarterly walk-forward reselection lowered Sharpe. Third, **2025–26 is a different regime**: the noise-area strategy's flat-to-negative run arrived after publication and a Quantpedia award. Post-publication decay is well documented in general, with anomaly returns 58% lower in the five years after publication ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/how-do-investment-strategies-perform-after-publication/)). The 0DTE-gamma explanation for the decay is speculation.

### Transfer to crypto and India hinges on fees per unit of R

On BTC/ETH/SOL perpetuals, a taker round trip costs 10–12 bp. That rules out any strategy whose per-trade edge resembles the 2–3 bp of US index intraday momentum. **The structure that fits crypto fees is few trades with wide R**: the noise-area or ORB family, where the stop sits far enough away that cost is a small fraction of risk. No published BTC/ETH/SOL backtest of the noise area, ORB or VWAP trend with perp fees and funding was found, so the transfer is untested. The sensible session anchors are the US cash open (13:30 UTC in US summer, 14:30 in winter), where BTC volume and volatility peak with the Europe-US overlap ([NYDIG](https://nydig.com/research/a-look-at-geographic-drivers-of-returns)); 00:00 UTC; and a volume-defined session following Shen et al. One time-of-day regularity can serve as a filter: buying BTC 21:00–23:00 UTC returned 33% annualised over 2015–2022, with costs not stated ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/)).

India's cost stack is harsher. Futures STT rose to 0.05% on the sell side from April 1 2026 ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/stt-hike-on-fo-to-take-effect-from-april-1-amid-rising-options-activity)). In the only public NSE test, a 15-minute ORB with a relative-volume filter grossed ₹173 on 759 trades against ₹2,357 of costs, and the first-30-minutes-predicts-last-30 signal captured about 1.5 bp against about 20 bp of cost ([GitHub](https://github.com/md0n-cmd/indian-equity-backtests)). Intraday "institutional" rules on NSE cash equities should be presumed dead until a variant shows a per-trade edge several times larger, for example only extreme relative-volume names. Nifty futures intraday momentum is a marginal candidate that has to clear about 0.075% per round trip.

## India's footprints arrive after the bell

### Exchange disclosures are real data with thin evidence

Indian retail education leans on footprints that US traders cannot see: delivery percentage, bulk and block deals, FII/DII flows and F&O open-interest build-ups. They are genuine institutional data. Almost all of them are published after the close, though, so the agent can only act at the next open, after the documented price reaction has usually happened.

The post-disclosure piece, which is the only part a rule-following agent can trade, is unmeasured. That makes these footprints good candidates for novel testing, since the project already ingests NSE bhavcopy. It also means the prior should be low. A 0.5% delivery round trip needs roughly a 1% gross drift over the holding period to pass a two-times-cost gate. Regime breaks complicate history: SEBI's late-2024 F&O changes and the April 2026 STT rise may have shifted any pre-2024 OI or PCR relation.

The cost arithmetic explains the ordering. On a crypto 5-minute setup with a 0.3% stop, a 12 bp round trip is **0.4R before any slippage**. On that arithmetic, tight-stop ICT entries on 1–5-minute bars give away most plausible edge before they start, which is why tests 4 and 9 carry an explicit cost-to-risk gate. The noise-area and trend designs hold positions for hours or weeks, with stops tied to session volatility or trend channels, so the same fee is a much smaller fraction of R. Kill zones and Power of 3 do not get their own test: kill zones enter tests 3 and 9 only as a pre-declared stratification, and AMD is the same event as a NY-midnight-anchored sweep in test 3.

## Validation must be harsher than the teachers

The SMC evidence base is itself the best argument for the protocol. Results that looked significant against zero vanished against random entries, and one significant t-statistic in 32 is what noise produces. The agent should apply seven steps to every row above.

**Step 1: reproduce before adapting.** Each transplant first reproduces a published number on the original market. Benchmarks: the SPY noise-area replication (Sharpe about 1.1 for 2020–24), the QQQ ORB trade count (1,775 against 1,795 in the paper, per the replicator), and the StatOasis daily SPY t-statistics for order blocks and FVGs. A mismatch of more than about 5% in trade count means a code bug, not an edge.

**Step 2: audit for lookahead.** Every pivot is usable only k bars after it forms. Every liquidity tolerance is trailing. Every session clock is in New York local time with daylight saving where ICT anchors to New York. No bar's own close may trigger an entry at that same bar's open. The `smartmoneyconcepts` package fails three of these four checks as shipped.

**Step 3: beat a matched random baseline, not zero.** For entry-trigger concepts, draw at least 1,000 random entries matched on instrument, time-of-day distribution, direction, holding-period rule and ATR-scaled stop and target. Report the strategy's percentile. For level concepts (FVG fills, sweeps, naked POCs, zones), compare against random levels at equal distance from price, following Osler's arbitrary-level bootstrap. Test 5 also needs the "random entry inside the trend" arm, which isolates the timing claim.

**Step 4: count every trial and correct for them.** Pre-register the full grid of concept × market × timeframe × variant cells before looking at the holdout, likely 100–200 cells for this programme. Control false discoveries with Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.10 across all cells, following Bajgrowicz and Scaillet. Apply White's Reality Check or Hansen's SPA to the best variant within each family, and require a deflated Sharpe ratio above 0.95 and t ≥ 3 for anything promoted.

**Step 5: keep an after-cost holdout that is used once.** Hold back October 2025 to September 2026. It conveniently covers the 2025–26 regime in which the noise area broke down. Run costs at 1× and 2×. The base costs are: perps 10–12 bp taker round trip plus actual funding; US ETFs and stocks $0.0035/share commission plus $0.01–0.02/share slippage; NSE intraday about 0.18%, delivery about 0.5%, Nifty futures about 0.075%. A strategy passes only if net expectancy is positive at 2× cost and gross edge per trade is at least twice the round-trip cost.

**Step 6: plan for sample size.** On my arithmetic, a strategy with per-trade standard deviation of about 1.5R needs n = (3 × 1.5/μ)² trades to reach t = 3. That is about 2,000 trades if the true edge is 0.1R, and about 225 if it is 0.3R. Daily-bar event studies on NSE footprints will need the whole cross-section to get there.

**Step 7: paper-trade forward.** Run at least three months or 100 trades on Delta, US and NSE paper fills before any live capital. Log realised slippage against the model, and log every rejected signal so that the filter uplift can be measured directly. Automatic kill rules: rolling 50-trade net expectancy below zero halves size; 100 trades below zero, or realised slippage above 1.5× the model, switches the strategy off.

## Conclusion

Testing these concepts moves the question from "do institutions leave footprints?" to "which footprints come from institutions that *must* trade a certain way?" The answer is narrow. Option dealers hedging gamma, benchmark-tracking execution and stop-loss orders resting beyond round numbers are constrained or mechanical participants, and the evidence for patterns around them is real, if thin and decaying. The ICT and SMC story of a deliberate "algorithm" that sweeps liquidity and then reverses has the mechanism backwards: the stop-cascade research and the FX evidence say a cleared stop cluster tends to keep going. For the agent, the most valuable SMC experiment is the sweep-direction test run both ways. A clear "continuation" result would turn the most popular retail idea into its own contrarian signal.

A second implication is about where to look rather than what to trade. Crypto perps and NSE stocks are the markets with no independent SMC, Wyckoff or order-flow tests at all, so the agent's own frozen, random-baseline, after-cost runs would be new evidence rather than replications. The prior should still be set by what is already known: across US stocks, ETFs and FX, every careful test has come back null or negative, nine in ten Indian F&O traders lose after costs, and the few edges that survive are measured in basis points. A programme that proves most of these concepts worthless on its own markets, quickly and cheaply, while keeping the two or three mechanical-flow strategies alive at small size, will have done its job.

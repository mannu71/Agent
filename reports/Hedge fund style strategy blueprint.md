# Engineer Survival First, Then Harvest Thin Edges

No trading strategy is foolproof or zero-risk, and an allocator should reject any agent that claims otherwise. Every lasting excess return is payment for bearing a risk, supplying liquidity or exploiting a small inefficiency that faster players also chase, and **87.7% of individual Indian F&O traders still lost money in FY26** after two years of regulatory tightening ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)). The most robust design open to an India-based retail trader gets its safety from structure, not prediction: a barbell with **70–80% of capital in a near-risk-free core (91-day T-bills near 5.3%, arbitrage funds near 6.2–6.5%)** and a 20–30% active book run as independent "pods" with hard drawdown stops, so the worst plausible trading year costs roughly one year of core income instead of the account. Inside that book the evidence supports slow, low-turnover edges with an economic explanation: Indian swing trades in 6–12-month momentum leaders near their 52-week highs behind a market-regime gate, long/flat time-series trend on BTC and ETH sized for India's 30%-with-no-loss-set-off crypto tax, and small wing-hedged Nifty option spreads. Naked option selling, cheap option buying, high-turnover intraday technical systems, retail market-making and crypto carry or arbitrage all fail after costs, taxes or tail events; intraday trading earns only one small index-futures pod, built on the documented end-of-day momentum effect and switched off until it clears the April 2026 STT increase. The screener is a ranking engine whose honest ceiling is **roughly 51–56% directional accuracy on liquid stocks**, so it must be judged on rank IC, precision@k against the base rate and expectancy after costs, logged immutably and disabled automatically when its edge decays. Realistic results are **about 6–9% a year on total capital in normal-to-good years, with a worst plausible year near −1% to −2%**, against 10–15% for the best multi-manager hedge funds, and proving even a Sharpe-1 edge takes about three years of live data. Almost every source figure came from search-engine summaries because direct page fetches were blocked during the research, so the final section grades which numbers to trust.

## Foolproof trading fails on arithmetic, evidence and history

In a competitive market a riskless positive excess return is an arbitrage, and arbitrages do not last. What survives is payment for bearing a priced risk (momentum crashes, carry crashes, short-volatility tails), payment for supplying liquidity, or a capacity-limited inefficiency that faster and larger players also hunt. All three lose money in some states of the world, and a retail agent must additionally out-earn brokerage, STT, exchange fees, GST, stamp duty and slippage, plus funding costs and a punitive tax regime in crypto. Losses also compound asymmetrically: recovering from a loss L needs a gain of L/(1−L), so a 20% drawdown needs +25% and a 50% drawdown needs +100%. "Low risk" can therefore only be defined operationally, as a bounded loss per trade, per day, per strategy and per year with a probability of ruin close to zero by construction, and every element of the blueprint below serves that definition.

The FY26 study is the most instructive. **82.1% of traders were loss-makers before costs and 87.7% after**, so transaction costs alone turned about **4.4 lakh gross winners into net losers**; average costs reached ₹31,628 per trader, options produced about 92% of individual losses, and about 97% of traders mainly bought options ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/sebi-futures-options-study-transaction-costs-traders-losses-fy26)). The pattern is global: fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders are predictably profitable ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/238220682_Do_Individual_Day_Traders_Make_Money_Evidence_from_Taiwan)), Taiwanese day traders lost 23.9 bps a day net of fees with aggregate losses in 14 of 15 years ([AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2019/preliminary/paper/ZKnGb4Zh)), and 97% of Brazilian futures day traders who persisted beyond 300 days lost money ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)).

The money retail loses does not vanish. In FY26, proprietary traders earned ₹44,483 crore and FPIs ₹13,896 crore gross in Indian derivatives, **about 99% of it through algorithms**, with roughly 75% of prop profits going to the top ten desks ([Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/sebi-fno-trader-losses-fy26-individual-derivatives)). A retail agent competes with those desks for the same small edges, which is why its design must avoid every contest decided by speed or fee tier.

### Even elite funds blow up when leverage, crowding or bugs line up

The best-run platforms still lose money in bursts. Millennium's hiring bar asked for a Sharpe ratio of 2.6 while "losing less than 5% at any one time" ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2021/02/how-to-get-a-job-at-hedge-fund-millennium)), yet in the March 2026 US–Iran war shock **Millennium lost 1.2–1.3%, Balyasny 4.3% and ExodusPoint 4.5%** in a single month ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/millennium-point72-rebound-iran-war-losses/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/new-york-brief/hedge-funds-walloped-by-a-month-of-turmoil-sparked-by-iran-war)), and BlackRock warned that pod ecosystems "may be far more crowded, correlated, and fragile than headline diversification metrics suggest" ([HedgeCo](https://www.hedgeco.net/news/04/2026/blackrock-issues-crowding-warning-for-hedge-funds.html)). History supplies sharper cases:

The damage usually arrives within hours to days, so de-risking must run automatically, and each control in this blueprint maps to one of those causes.

### "Safe" assets and convincing backtests both fail

Assets sold as safe are only relatively safe. **Arbitrage funds returned 3.61% in calendar 2026 to July** against about 6% trailing one-year ([INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/mutual-funds/hybrid/arbitrage-funds)); Nifty futures traded at a steep discount to spot in March 2020, inverting cash-and-carry trades ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/news-cm/nifty-march-futures-trade-at-steep-discount-120031601062_1.html)); USDC fell to about $0.87 for three days in March 2023 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/218993/usdc-and-dai-remain-at-about-0-90-following-circles-disclosure-of-funds-at-svb)); and WazirX, after a $230m hack, kept Indian users' funds frozen for about 15 months before a restructuring ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/10/13/wazirx-restructuring-cleared-in-massive-relief-for-usd230m-hack-victims)).

Backtests mislead more reliably still. **Across 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe explained less than 2.5% of out-of-sample Sharpe (R² < 0.025)**, while volatility and drawdown predicted live results better ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)). Published anomalies earn **26% less out of sample and 58% less after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)), and after only **seven configurations** a researcher should expect at least one two-year backtest with a Sharpe above 1 even when the true Sharpe is zero ([Bailey et al.](https://carmamaths.org/jon/backtest.pdf)). Proof also takes time. On the note authors' calculations from the minimum-track-record formula ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://ideas.repec.org/a/rsk/journ4/2223785.html)), a true Sharpe of 2 becomes statistically significant within months, a Sharpe of 1 needs **about 2.7–3 years**, and a Sharpe of 0.5 needs **about 11 years**. A record that is too smooth is itself a warning: Madoff's feeder fund showed a Sharpe of 3.4 on 3% volatility with only seven small losing months in 14.5 years ([SEC OIG](https://sec.gov/news/studies/2009/oig-509/exhibit-0221.pdf)), so an agent producing that profile should be presumed to have a look-ahead or pricing bug until proven otherwise.

## A barbell holds 70–80% in a 5–6.5% core and caps the damage

### Every sleeve must beat 5.3% risk-free and 6.5% arbitrage

The hurdles for every active strategy, as of late 2026, are set by the cheapest safe alternatives:

| Benchmark | Rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| RBI repo rate (Aug 2026) | **5.25%** | [Traders Union](https://tradersunion.com/news/central-banks/show/2830177-rbi-treasury-bill-auction-91-364-day/) |
| 91-day T-bill | **5.25–5.34%** | [Traders Union](https://tradersunion.com/news/central-banks/show/2830177-rbi-treasury-bill-auction-91-364-day/) |
| Overnight funds, one year | 4.83–4.98% | [ArthGyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/fund/lic-mf-overnight-fund-direct-growth/147515) |
| Arbitrage funds, one year (Aug–Sep 2026) | **6.18–6.50%** | [ArthGyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/fund/quant-arbitrage-fund-direct-growth/153426) |
| Arbitrage funds, three years | 7.15% | [ArthGyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/fund/compare/tata-arbitrage-fund-direct-growth-vs-baroda-bnp-paribas-arbitrage-fund-direct-growth-option/145724-vs-150251) |

Arbitrage funds hold at least 65% hedged equity and are therefore taxed as equity funds ([INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/mutual-funds/hybrid/arbitrage-funds)). They are essentially a professionally run cash-futures basis trade, which makes them the right comparison for any market-neutral sleeve and a hurdle that a retail agent doing the same trade directly would struggle to clear after STT and slab-rate business tax.

Professional comparators set the ceiling. Millennium returned 10.5% and Citadel 10.2% in 2025 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/2025-returns-hedge-funds-rolling-234728986.html)); through August 2026 Citadel was **+12.1%**, Millennium **+8.2%**, ExodusPoint +4.9% and Balyasny +0.5% year to date ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/citadel-millennium-and-peers-post-muted-august-returns/)). Indian long-short Specialised Investment Funds (SIFs) had returned **+4.87% to +10.85% since launch** by July 2026 ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/mutual-funds/equity-vs-hybrid-long-short-si-fs-how-have-20-funds-performed-since-launch-july-2026/article-197453/)), and Category III long-short AIFs posted a median +1.2% in September 2026 with 29 of 32 funds positive ([PMS AIF World](https://www.pmsaifworld.com/wp-content/uploads/PMS-AIF-WORLD-Sept-2026-CAT-3-AIF-Newsletter.pdf)). Indian regulation also offers leverage sanity checks: Category III AIFs may not exceed **2× NAV** ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/1375094611151.pdf)) and SIFs may carry at most **25% unhedged short exposure** ([NISM](https://www.nism.ac.in/revamp-nism/mutual-funds/specialized-investment-funds-taking-stock-of-what-new-they-offer/)). The practical point is blunt: a trader with ₹10 lakh can buy an SIF and anyone can buy an arbitrage fund, so the agent's active book is only justified if its live record beats those alternatives after every cost and tax.

### The allocation is a barbell with pods on the risky end

Taleb's barbell puts 85–90% in ultra-safe assets and 10–15% in risk-seeking bets so that the worst case is a bounded haircut ([RBC Direct Investing](https://www.rbcdirectinvesting.com/learn/en/di/hubs/ideas-and-motivation/article/barbell-and-other-investing-strategies/mrqeu0ww)). Multi-manager platforms run each book on a tight stop; Millennium reportedly halves a pod's capital at a 5% drawdown and closes it at 7.5%, though that rule comes only from a secondary source ([Young and Calculated](https://youngandcalculated.substack.com/p/what-senior-pms-at-multi-managers)). The blueprint combines the two ideas:

| Bucket | Share of total capital (central, range) | Instruments | Job | Drawdown rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core | **75%** (70–80%) | 91-day T-bills, overnight/liquid funds, arbitrage funds | Earn the hurdle, fund trading drawdowns | None; rebalance yearly |
| Broker cash buffer | 5% (3–7%) | Cash at broker | Margin, mark-to-market, the 50%-cash collateral rule | — |
| Sleeve A: India swing momentum/trend | **13%** (8–15%) | Long-only delivery equity in liquid Nifty 200 / F&O-universe stocks | Main return engine | Halve at −7.5%, off at −15% from the sleeve's high |
| Sleeve B: crypto trend | **4%** (2–6%) | BTC/ETH long/flat via INR-settled perps at ≤1× or low-turnover spot | Diversifier with some crisis convexity | Same |
| Sleeve C: defined-risk index options | **3%** (0–4%), as a max-loss budget | Nifty/Sensex iron condors, iron flies, credit spreads | Harvest the volatility premium with capped tails | Same |
| Sleeve D: intraday index-futures momentum | **0% until validated**, then ≤4% | Nifty futures, at most one trade a day, flat by close | Prove-it pod | 1% of active book daily loss limit; same sleeve rule |
| Active book total | **25%** | | | Halve all at −10%, stop all at −20% from the active-book high |

Capital shares are starting points; the real allocation is by risk. Each sleeve is volatility-targeted so that its bootstrapped 95th-percentile 12-month drawdown sits inside its −15% stop, each sleeve's P&L is measured net of its market beta (Nifty or BTC) the way pods are judged, and sleeve weights come from inverse volatility or hierarchical risk parity, with any performance-based tilt bounded to ±50% of the risk-parity weight and updated no faster than weekly because short performance windows are mostly noise. Diversification is the one reliable free lunch, but it is smaller than people hope: for N strategies with equal Sharpe S and average correlation ρ, the portfolio Sharpe is S·√(N/(1+(N−1)ρ)), so four uncorrelated sleeves double the Sharpe while at ρ = 0.5 they add only about 26%, and correlations rise in crises.

Minimum sizes constrain the design further. One Nifty lot (65 units at about 23,100) is about **₹15 lakh notional**, by SEBI's design of ₹15–20 lakh contracts ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sebi-announces-six-key-changes-to-curb-speculation-in-derivatives-trading-124100101316_1.html); [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/nifty-lot-size-2026-bank-nifty-sensex)), so running Sleeve D at 1× leverage needs about ₹15 lakh in that pod alone; below roughly ₹10–15 lakh of active capital, Sleeve D is infeasible and Sleeve C shrinks to single-lot, narrow-wing spreads. Fixed costs bite small accounts too: a Kite Connect data plan costs ₹500 a month ([Marketcalls](https://www.marketcalls.in/fintech/zerodha-makes-trading-api-free-for-personal-use-bundles-historical-data-with-connect-api.html)), and that ₹6,000 a year is a 1.2% drag on a ₹5 lakh active book but only 0.24% on ₹25 lakh.

### Realistic returns: single digits with a bounded worst year

The table below is arithmetic illustration, not forecast. It assumes a 75/25 split, a core earning 5.5%, and active-book figures stated after trading costs and trading taxes:

| Scenario | Active book (net) | Core | Total portfolio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bad year: active book hits its −20% stop | −20% | 5.5% | **≈ −0.9%** |
| Bad year with gap overshoot to −25% | −25% | 5.5% | ≈ −2.1% |
| Active book flat | 0% | 5.5% | ≈ 4.1% |
| Base case | +8% | 5.5% | **≈ 6.1%** |
| Good year | +20% | 5.5% | **≈ 9.1%** |

In the base case the active book adds only about two percentage points over the core alone, so the agent's value lies in the upside option if a sleeve proves a real edge and scales, and in keeping losses bounded while that edge is tested. A realistic live Sharpe for the active book is **0.5–1.0** after the standard 50% haircut practitioners apply to backtested Sharpe ratios ([Harvey & Liu](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF)). Under a normal approximation the probability that the active book trails cash in any year is Φ(−Sharpe), which is **about 31% at a Sharpe of 0.5 and about 16% at 1.0**, so losing years are part of the plan rather than evidence of failure. Indian long-short professionals currently earn mid-single digits to low teens with drawdowns of roughly 5–10%, which supports a target of **8–12% a year on the active book with a 15–20% maximum drawdown**; any backtest promising 30%+ with single-digit drawdowns should be treated as overfit until proven live.

## Slow, cost-light edges earn a place; crowded and convex ones do not

### Sleeve A: buy Indian leadership, time the pullback, size for the gap

Momentum is the best-documented swing-to-position effect in India:

| Evidence | Return | Drawdowns | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| IIMA survivorship-corrected momentum factor, 1994–2014 | **About 21.9% a year** | **−62% in 2000; −52.8% in 2008–09** | [IIMA](https://web.iima.ac.in/~iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/drawdown.php) |
| Nifty200 Momentum 30 TRI, since 2005 | **18.8% CAGR**, vs 13.9% for the Nifty 200 TRI | **−67.9% in 2008; −31.79% from Sep 2024 to Apr 2025** | [HDFC MF](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf) |

Over the September 2024 to April 2025 stretch, while the momentum index fell 31.79%, the Nifty 50 fell only 15.35% ([DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/active-momentum-funds-timing-the-market-or-tapping-the-trend-51070)). A 2026 practitioner backtest, net of brokerage, slippage and capital-gains tax, exposes the deeper catch ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/)):

| Portfolio | Net CAGR | Max drawdown |
|---|---|---|
| Base 30-stock momentum portfolio | **14.60%** | **−70.61%** |
| Less-liquid half | 19.43% | −66.41% |
| Liquid half | **8.51%** | −75.09% |
| Nifty 50 | 10.41% | −55.12% |

The alpha sat in exactly the names an agent struggles to trade at size, which also carry circuit-limit and surveillance risk. Proximity to the 52-week high gave a **more stable alpha than standard 12-1 momentum, with weaker long-term reversals**, in Indian data from 2004 to 2023 ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/an-analysis-of-52-weeks-high-effect-on-indian-stocks/)). Momentum crashes cluster in "panic states" after market declines with high volatility, and a volatility-scaled version roughly **doubles momentum's Sharpe ratio** ([Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)), which is the theoretical basis for this sleeve's regime gate.

Horizon shapes the rules. At 1-month horizons Indian stocks show reversal rather than momentum ([Publishing India](https://www.publishingindia.com/archive/ijfm/a-study-of-contrarian-and-momentum-profits-in-indian-stock-market)), so ranking on last week's return is the wrong tool; the robust design uses 6–12-month momentum and 52-week-high proximity to select the universe and uses short-term weakness only to time entry. Fixed short holding periods throw away the profit engine: in the largest modern stock trend study, **fewer than 7% of trades produced most of the profit** and the average trade lasted about 305 days ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/does-trend-following-still-work-on-stocks/)), so swing exits need a trailing-stop "runner" rule rather than fixed short targets. Classic short-term mean reversion has decayed, too; CXO's live review of a Connors product found the gross win rate falling from 62% to 55% and underperformance against SPY after costs ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-gurus/review-of-larry-connors-daily-battle-plan/)).

Results season is a risk event, not an edge. US post-earnings drift largely disappeared for non-microcaps by about 2006 ([Martineau](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)), a 2014–2018 study of 100 NSE firms found no exploitable drift ([Trends Economics & Management](https://journals.vutbr.cz/index.php/trends/article/view/541)), and Indian results can land mid-session within 30 minutes of a board meeting ending ([StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/faqs-on-sebi-lodr-amendments-dated-05th-may-2021-102028.html)), so the sleeve cuts size or exits before results. Delivery percentage, widely promoted by Indian brokers, has no peer-reviewed predictive evidence and earns at most a secondary-filter role. Index rebalances deserve a calendar entry: the December 2025 Nifty200 Momentum 30 reshuffle implied about **₹16,130 crore of round-trip flows**, exceeding a day's volume in 27 stocks ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/nifty200-momentum30-a-flip-of-index-switch-jolts-16k-cr-across-the-grid-125121400388_1.html)).

The concrete rules follow from that evidence. The universe is NSE EQ-series stocks above a price floor and a median 20-day traded-value floor, excluding names under ASM, GSM or ESM surveillance, the trade-for-trade series and stocks with 2% or 5% price bands ([Bajaj Broking](https://bajajbroking.in/blog/decoding-asm-and-gsm-frameworks-and-stages); [Chittorgarh](https://www.chittorgarh.com/report/stock-nse-circuit-filters/120/20/)). The screener's composite score (next section) picks the top quintile, entries come on a 2–5-day pullback or a breakout from a tight base, the initial stop sits at 2–3× ATR(14) and converts to a trailing stop for winners, positions face a roughly six-week time limit unless the trailing stop is still rising, the book holds 8–15 names under a sector cap, and full size is allowed only when the regime gate is green.

Costs are moderate but real. A ₹1 lakh delivery round trip costs about **₹238 (0.24%)**, dominated by the 0.1% STT on each side, and a ₹25,000 trade about 0.29% because the depository charge is fixed (note author's arithmetic from the [Zerodha schedule](https://stockcalc.in/blog/zerodha-brokerage-charges-2026-full-breakdown)); short-term gains then pay 20% tax ([Quicko](https://learn.quicko.com/ltcg-tax-equity-shares)). No rigorous net-of-cost test of a 2–6-week Indian swing system exists, so the planning ranges below are the note authors' synthesis:

| Trade type | Win rate | Payoff | Net expectancy per trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trend/breakout | 35–45% | 2–3× | About 0.5–1.5% |
| Pullback | 55–65% | 0.7–1.2× | About 0.3–1.0% |

The sleeve must also plan for a **20–35% drawdown in an ordinary correction and 50%+ in a 2008-type crash without a regime filter**, which is why its stop and regime gate are not optional.

### Sleeve B: long/flat crypto trend on majors, sized for the tax code

Crypto shows strong time-series momentum at daily and weekly horizons ([Liu & Tsyvinski](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3226806)). An ensemble of Donchian-channel trend models with volatility sizing on a survivorship-free top-20 coin rotation reported a **net-of-fees Sharpe above 1.5 and 10.8% annual alpha over BTC** for 2015–2025 ([Zarattini, Pagani & Barbon](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5209907)), and earlier crypto trend studies report Sharpe ratios of roughly 0.5–1.5 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12155)). Cross-sectional altcoin momentum is much weaker evidence: its alpha comes largely from short positions and costly trading ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1904.00890)), it suffers severe crashes in which a single coin can make results insignificant ([Grobys et al.](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/handle/10024/20018)), and small, illiquid coins reverse at weekly horizons while large, liquid ones trend ([Fičura & Colak](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4378429)). The sleeve therefore trades BTC and ETH, plus at most a few of the most liquid coins, long or flat, with weekly decisions.

India's tax code is the binding constraint. Gains on virtual digital assets are taxed at 30% plus 4% cess, only the cost of acquisition is deductible, losses cannot be set off or carried forward, and a 1% TDS applies to transfers ([CalcGuru](https://calcguru.in/crypto-vda-tax-india-30-percent-194s/)); under the Income-tax Act 2025 the TDS moves to s.393 and exchange reporting to s.509 ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/crypto-vda-taxation-income-tax-act-1961-vs-2025-framework.html); [CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/09/21/india-crypto-tax-2026-30-vda-levy-1-tds-stay-as-section-285baa-reporting-goes-live/)). The note authors' worked example shows the bite: a trend system with a 40% win rate, +15% average win and −5% average loss expects **+3.0% per trade before tax but only about +1.13%** if each winner is taxed at about 31.2% and losers give no relief, so low-win-rate, high-payoff trend systems suffer most. TDS is a cash-flow drag rather than a final cost, but turning capital over 30–50 times a year locks **30–50% of capital** in TDS credits until refund, and the 2022 regime drove up to **81% of Indian exchange volume** offshore ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/headlines/indian-crypto-exchanges-lose-us1-2-trillion/)).

INR-settled futures on FIU-registered Indian venues are marketed as business income outside both the 30% VDA tax and TDS ([Delta Exchange India](https://guides.delta.exchange/delta-exchange-india-user-guide)), but practitioners call that classification unsettled ([CA in Delhi](https://www.caindelhiindia.com/blog/crypto-futures-taxation/)). **The robust rule is to use whichever wrapper the trader's chartered accountant approves, but to switch the sleeve on only if its backtest stays positive under the harshest plausible treatment**: 30% plus cess on every winning trade, no offset for losers, and 1% TDS on every spot sale.

Venue plumbing completes the picture. Delta Exchange India charges 0.02% maker and 0.05% taker on perpetuals plus 18% GST ([Delta API docs](https://docs.delta.exchange/); [Tradelize](https://tradelize.com/crypto-exchange-reviews/delta-exchange-review/)) and CoinDCX 0.025% and 0.07% ([TradersUnion](https://tradersunion.com/brokers/crypto/view/coindcx/fees/)); only FIU-registered exchanges are lawful for residents, and LRS forbids remitting margin to offshore venues ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/ScriptS/FAQDisplay.aspx?Id=115)). Funding on long perpetuals belongs in the backtest: the common 0.01%-per-8-hours baseline equals **10.95% a year**, funding averaged only about +2.1% a year in September 2026 ([DefiRate](https://defirate.com/perp/)), and it reached nearly 30% annualised in the run-up to the October 2025 crash ([FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)). India's USDT premium over the bank dollar rate has ranged from 3% to above 8.5% ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/usdt-premium-india-8-5-percent-supply/)), so the agent must check how its venue marks USDT in INR. Counterparty risk is concrete: WazirX lost about $230m and froze funds for about 15 months ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/10/13/wazirx-restructuring-cleared-in-massive-relief-for-usd230m-hack-victims)), and CoinDCX lost $44m from a hot wallet in July 2025 ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/web3/2025/07/19/indian-crypto-exchange-coindcx-suffers-44m-hack)). The sleeve therefore runs at **≤1× effective leverage with a 2× hard cap**, keeps collateral in INR rather than synthetic stablecoins, splits balances across at least two venues, sweeps profits to the bank, and treats funding rates purely as a crowding gauge, since BIS research finds that **high carry predicts crashes** ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1087-crypto-carry)).

### Sleeve C: rent the volatility premium only with wings on

A volatility premium exists in Nifty options. One study found India VIX averaging 16.59 against 14.12 realised volatility, a premium of **+2.46 vol points that was positive on 79.7% of days**, but its worst day (5 March 2020) was **−64.5 points, about 26 times the average**; that study is a non-peer-reviewed GitHub project ([RajolKumar2003](https://github.com/RajolKumar2003/volatility-risk-premium-india)). SEBI's FY26 data show option sellers were the only strategy group with a positive median return on capital ([Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/business/sebi-fno-loss-study-explained-why-9-in-10-retail-traders-lost-91685-crore-in-fy26)), yet the tails explain why naked selling is still unsafe. Some naked sellers lost more than their posted margin in March 2020 ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/what-happened-with-options-trader-in-march-2020/96234)); India VIX hit a record low as voting began in April 2024, just before the June 4 results-day crash, so sellers were paid least before the biggest move ([Yahoo/Bloomberg](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/indias-key-equity-risk-guage-033032867.html)); and SEBI's 2025 interim order against Jane Street alleged that expiry-day index levels were pushed in favour of a large short-options book that earned about ₹36,500 crore of net profit ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/04/indian-regulator-bars-us-trading-firm-jane-street-from-accessing-securities-market.html); [Oxford Business Law Blog](https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/oblb/blog-post/2025/07/jane-street-and-expiry-day-trap-unpacking-sebis-crackdown-algorithmic)).

The rules have shifted as well. Weekly options now exist only on Nifty (NSE, Tuesday expiry) and Sensex (BSE, Thursday expiry) ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/sebi-approves-nses-derivatives-expiry-to-tuesday-bses-to-thursday-lead--20250617171804)); short options carry an extra 2% extreme-loss margin on expiry day, when the calendar-spread margin benefit also disappears ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-strengthens-index-derivative-rules)); and STT on option premium rose to 0.15% from April 2026 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/budget-2026-fm-sitharaman-raises-stt-on-fo-trades-signals-tighter-market-oversight-513932-2026-02-01)). One option lot costs about **₹62.5 per round trip** on the note author's arithmetic ([Rupeezy](https://support.rupeezy.in/support/solutions/articles/2100044912-what-are-nse-transaction-and-other-charges-as-per-exchange-and-government)), so a four-leg iron condor costs roughly ₹200–250 per lot when every leg is closed, a meaningful share of a small credit.

Sleeve C therefore trades only defined-risk iron condors, iron flies or credit spreads, caps each expiry's maximum loss at 0.5–1% of the active book, opens nothing on expiry day and closes everything before the final hours (sidestepping both expiry-day manipulation and STT on exercised in-the-money options), and stands aside for elections, the Union Budget, RBI policy, major global binary events and whenever the volatility gate is red. No net-of-cost backtest of retail Nifty iron condors was found, so the sleeve is model-based and must earn its place through the validation gates.

### Sleeve D: one structurally explained intraday trade, off by default

Most intraday edges vanish after Indian costs:

The exception has a mechanism behind it. The return from the previous close through the first half-hour predicts the **last half-hour's return** in the US with a predictive R² of 1.6% ([Gao et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2440866)), in more than 60 futures markets where it is linked to option dealers' gamma hedging ([Baltussen et al.](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)), in Indian equity futures according to an unrefereed working paper ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383567351_Hedging_Demand_and_Intraday_Momentum_within_the_Indian_Stock_Market)), and in Bitcoin ([Shen et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/fire.12290)). A more active US version reported a Sharpe of 1.33 net of costs over 2007–2024 ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)), but it is unreplicated after publication and its authors have commercial ties.

Costs and tax both point to index futures as the vehicle. After STT on futures rose from 0.02% to 0.05% in April 2026 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/budget-2026-fm-sitharaman-raises-stt-on-fo-trades-signals-tighter-market-oversight-513932-2026-02-01)), a Nifty futures round trip costs about **₹970, roughly 15 index points or 0.06%** (note author's arithmetic). Intraday cash equity is **speculative** income whose losses offset only speculative income and carry forward four years, whereas F&O is **non-speculative** business income whose losses carry forward eight years ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/income-tax-on-f-and-o-trading-in-india-complete-guide-for-fy-2025-26)). Sleeve D therefore trades Nifty futures only, makes at most one decision a day, acts only when the early move is large or volatility is high (when the effect is strongest), and manages its own exit to be flat by the close, because broker auto square-off of intraday positions around 15:20–15:25 clips the last half-hour ([Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/sebi-new-margin-rules-intraday-trading)). It switches on only if the conditional last-half-hour move exceeds twice the round-trip cost in walk-forward tests on post-April-2026 data. Crypto intraday fares no better: 15-minute reversal appears in 90% of Binance pairs ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)), but Indian perpetual taker costs of about 0.12–0.17% round trip with GST probably exceed the typical move, leaving only maker-only execution, and spot intraday trading is impossible under the TDS regime.

### Tail protection: shrink exposure before buying puts

Protective puts look attractive after a crash, but the long-run evidence favours simply holding less exposure. Universa reported +3,612% in March 2020 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-04-08/taleb-advised-universa-tail-risk-fund-returned-3-600-in-march)), a return on a small premium allocation that is unaudited, while AQR research concluded that puts are "quite ineffective at reducing drawdowns versus the simple alternative of statically reducing exposure" ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/do-protective-equity-index-puts-work-well/)). Retail sources put blanket hedging at 2–5% of portfolio value a year ([Gotrade](https://heygotrade.com/en/blog/protective-put-portfolio-insurance-tail-risk-hedging/)), and the note authors' Black-Scholes model (not market quotes) estimated that rolling one-month 5%-out-of-the-money Nifty puts costs about 0.9–4.4% a year. Trend following supplies slow-crisis protection but not reliably: the Barclay CTA Index gained about 14% in 2008 ([BarclayHedge](https://barclayhedge.com/insider/pr_oct_09_2008)) and the SG CTA and Trend indices rose 20.1% and 27.3% in 2022 ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/flat-december-sees-ctas-complete-record-year-2022/)), yet the SG Trend Index fell **20.4% between May 2024 and May 2025** ([Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/insight/does-trend-followings-recent-struggle-signal-that-the-strategy-is-structurally-broken/)). The protection stack therefore runs in priority order: size and leverage control first, defined-risk structures for any short option second, the crypto trend sleeve as a partial crisis diversifier third, and opportunistic put spreads last, bought when India VIX is low (10.3–12.6 in late September 2026, per [JM Financial](https://www.jmfinancialservices.in/market-news-and-insights/1733807)) or ahead of scheduled binary events.

## The screener ranks candidates and must earn trust in its own log

### Build it on the few signals that survive

When machine-learning methods are compared on stock prediction, they agree on the dominant short-horizon signals: **momentum, liquidity and volatility** ([Gu, Kelly & Xiu](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3159577)). In India, NSE builds its momentum index from volatility-adjusted 6- and 12-month returns ([Axis Max Life](https://www.axismaxlife.com/blog/investments/what-is-nifty-200-momentum-30-index)), and Nifty200 Momentum 30 returned a 17.6% CAGR against 15.3% for the quality index over 2005–2026, though its pre-2020 history is back-calculated ([Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/media/pms/newsletter/wright_momentum_quality_note_april_26.pdf)); 52-week-high proximity carries its own Indian evidence (Sleeve A). The high-volume return premium, by contrast, is weak in India and significant only at the 10% level when value-weighted ([Singh, Wang & Hua](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pacfin/v91y2025ics0927538x2500126x.html)), so volume surges confirm but never select. In crypto, the market, size and 1–4-week momentum factors explain the cross-section ([Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3379131)), but only large, liquid coins trend.

Construction follows index-provider practice. MSCI winsorizes each raw factor, z-scores it within the universe, caps z-scores at ±3 and averages them ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/eqb/methodology/meth_docs/MSCI_Quality_Indexes_Methodology_May2022.pdf)); the screener does the same after applying the surveillance and price-band exclusions, ranks what remains, and lets the regime gate decide how many names to act on. A machine-learning upgrade is justified, but only modestly. Out of sample in emerging markets over 2002–2021, long-short returns were about 0.8% a month for linear models, 1.0% for tree models and 1.2% for neural networks and ensembles ([Robeco](https://robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2023/10/using-machine-learning-for-emerging-market-equity-returns)); machine learning beat OLS on a survivorship-free Indian sample ([Lalwani & Meshram](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00036846.2021.1982132)); and learning-to-rank roughly tripled the Sharpe of cross-sectional momentum in one study ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07149)). The sensible path is a transparent z-score baseline first, then LightGBM or LambdaRank on the same features under purged walk-forward validation, expecting an uplift of about 0.2–0.4% a month long-short.

The screener runs in three modes: an end-of-day NSE swing ranking computed after the close for next-day entries; a weekly crypto ranking on explicit UTC day boundaries; and an intraday watchlist of F&O stocks, which have no hard price circuit ([Rupeezy](https://support.rupeezy.in/support/solutions/articles/21000004845-what-are-circuit-limits-or-price-bands)), kept in research-only logging until validated because no peer-reviewed Indian intraday ranking evidence exists. Every suggestion carries its rank and score, a calibrated probability of beating the universe median over the stated horizon shown next to the base rate, the entry rule, ATR stop, size and horizon, and the regime state at the time.

Data quality decides whether any of this works. Survivor-only Indian small-cap backtests overstate returns by **4.94 percentage points a year** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19380)), so the universe must be rebuilt from NSE bhavcopy archives, which include delisted names, using tools such as jugaad-data ([GitHub](https://github.com/jugaad-py/jugaad-data)). yfinance mishandled the HDFC Bank demerger ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/has-anyone-found-wrong-adjusted-prices-in-nse-historical-data-for-backtesting/192938)) and belongs only in prototypes. Kite Connect serves minute candles 60 days per request at 3 requests a second ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/8899/rate-limit-of-kite-connect-historical-data-api-and-lookback)), Fyers offers minute data from mid-2017 and Dhan five years of intraday history ([StocksDeveloper](https://stocksdeveloper.in/documentation/supported-brokers/api-comparison/market-data/)), and CoinGecko's free tier rose to 100 requests a minute in May 2026 ([CoinGecko](https://docs.coingecko.com/changelog.md)). No independent study of popular Indian scanners such as Chartink, Streak or Trendlyne was found ([Strike.money](https://www.strike.money/reviews/chartink)), so a custom screener's edge comes from data hygiene and validation, not novel indicators.

### Expect 51–56%, distrust anything above 60%

Credible, leakage-controlled models reach only modest accuracy. An LSTM on S&P 500 constituents achieved **almost 55%** next-day directional accuracy, worth 0.46% a day with a Sharpe of 5.8 before costs, yet its profitability fluctuated around zero after costs from 2010 ([Fischer & Krauss](https://iwf.rw.fau.de/files/2015/12/11-2017.pdf)); classifiers on the most liquid cryptocurrencies reached **about 55–65%** ([Akyildirim et al.](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/items/8f518a9d-24b8-4b2a-a6ea-f17847399ccd/full)). Claims well above that are red flags: one NIFTY 50 study reports **87–90%** trend accuracy, and its accuracy jumps when data are "reorganized", the classic signature of label leakage ([CUP repository](https://kr.cup.edu.in/handle/32116/2699)), while ordinary specification search produces "significant" backtests even on synthetic data with zero predictability ([Nikolopoulos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15531)).

The link between information coefficient (IC) and hit rate shows why honest numbers are small. Under bivariate normality the sign hit rate is 0.5 + arcsin(IC)/π (note author's derivation), so an IC of 0.05 yields only **51.6%** across the whole universe and an IC of 0.10 yields 53.2%. A mean IC above 0.02 is a common practitioner bar ([sharpely](https://sharpely.in/knowledge-base/alphalab/decoding-alpha-analysis-report)), and published daily LightGBM benchmarks show rank ICs of about 0.024–0.058 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18850)). The fundamental law, IR ≈ IC × √breadth, explains why small ICs still pay, but only with breadth ([CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/capital-markets/fundamental-law-of-active-management/)): an IC of 0.02 across 6,000 independent bets a year gives an information ratio of **about 1.55**, whereas an IC of 0.05 across the 50 bets of a concentrated swing trader gives **about 0.35**. A retail screener showing 5–10 picks a week is low-breadth by construction, and its owner should expect long losing stretches.

### Score it the way a user experiences it

Hit rate alone misleads, as three profiles with similar headline appeal show:

| Profile | Win rate | Payoff | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trend system | 35% | 3:1 | **+0.40R per trade** |
| "Accurate" screener | 55% | 1.0 win vs 1.3 loss | **Loses** |
| Option seller | 90% | 1 win vs 12 loss | **Loses** |

OptionSellers.com, whose win rate was "often north of 75% on a monthly basis", told 290 clients in 2018 that all their money was gone ([Early Retirement Now](https://earlyretirementnow.com/2018/12/18/the-optionsellers-debacle/)). The scorecard therefore reports, per horizon and per regime, the rank IC and ICIR computed the Alphalens way ([QuantRocket](https://www.quantrocket.com/codeload/quant-finance-lectures/quant_finance_lectures/Lecture38-Factor-Analysis-with-Alphalens.ipynb.html)); the top-minus-bottom quantile spread and whether returns rise monotonically across quantiles; precision@k against the base rate, since "60% of picks went up" shows no skill in a week when 60% of all stocks rose; expectancy, profit factor and payoff ratio after costs and slippage; Brier score and calibration error for the stated probabilities; and turnover alongside live versus modelled slippage.

Statistical patience is part of the design. On the note authors' calculations with a one-sided test at 80% power, distinguishing a 55% hit rate from 50% takes **about 620 independent trades** and distinguishing 53% takes **about 1,700**. **After 100 trades, a 55% screener cannot be told apart from a coin flip** (±9.8 points at 95% confidence), and because same-day picks are correlated, effective sample sizes sit closer to the number of distinct periods, pushing swing-screener proof into many months or years.

### Keep it honest after launch

Edges decay, and the plan should assume they shrink by one-third to two-thirds after going live, in line with the McLean–Pontiff declines ([McLean & Pontiff](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)); industry estimates put the cost of alpha decay at about **5.6% a year in the US** ([Maven Securities](https://www.mavensecurities.com/alpha-decay-what-does-it-look-like-and-what-does-it-mean-for-systematic-traders/)). Maintenance is therefore an operating process. It starts with an immutable suggestion log: each suggestion is written to append-only, hash-chained storage before its outcome exists, recording model version, feature-snapshot hash, generation and data-as-of timestamps, symbol, score, stated probability, entry rule, stop, horizon and the base rate at that moment, and a separate job scores outcomes later. Rolling rank IC, precision@k, expectancy and calibration are computed over 20-, 60- and 120-day windows. A population stability index on features and scores runs weekly, alerting above 0.1 and acting at 0.25 ([Qlik](https://help.qlik.com/en-US/cloud-services/Subsystems/Hub/Content/Sense_Hub/AutoML/monitor-drift.htm)), while CUSUM and Page-Hinkley change-point tests watch daily rank IC ([Menelaus](https://menelaus.readthedocs.io/en/latest/menelaus.change_detection.html)). A signal family is disabled automatically when its 60-day rank IC turns negative alongside a CUSUM alarm, when its 120-day expectancy after costs turns negative, or when live drawdown exceeds 1.5–2× the backtest maximum. Models retrain monthly for swing and weekly for intraday, plus on drift alarms, and new models run as shadow challengers that replace the champion only after beating it on forward data by a pre-set margin.

Leakage gets its own smoke tests: shifting every feature forward by one bar should collapse performance, shuffling labels within each date should drop accuracy to the base rate, and running the whole pipeline on random-walk prices should find nothing. Every value carries an `available_at` timestamp in UTC, because US data dated the same calendar day is in the future for an Indian close-of-day decision, and any LLM-derived feature is look-ahead contaminated for periods before the model's training cutoff.

## Deterministic limits, regime gates and stress tests bound the downside

### Size for the gap, not the stop

Position size comes from four layers applied in sequence. Fixed-fractional risk sets the base: each trade risks 0.25–0.5% of the active book at a stop placed at k·ATR(14) with k between 1.5 and 3, 1% is a hard maximum, and a trade whose single F&O lot already exceeds the budget is skipped rather than oversized. A gap cap then limits any single stock to 5% of the active book, because Indian history shows stops can execute 5–10% beyond their level on an index and 20–27% on a single stock; IndusInd Bank fell 27% in a day in March 2025 ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/indusind-bank-crashes-27-percentage-on-severe-discrepancies-in-derivatives-portfolio/76611.html)) and Adani Enterprises 23% in November 2024 ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/adani-enterprises-shares-crack-23-today-heres-what-analysts-say-454490-2024-11-21)). A portfolio volatility overlay comes next and serves as a risk control rather than an alpha source: volatility targeting reliably thins left tails across asset classes but raises Sharpe mainly for risk assets ([Quantpedia on Harvey et al.](https://quantpedia.com/the-impact-of-volatility-targeting-on-equities-bonds-commodities-and-currencies)), and a real-time test across 103 equity strategies found no systematic improvement, with gains mainly for momentum, profitability and betting-against-beta ([Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). Finally, a Kelly ceiling at one-quarter Kelly caps everything: full Kelly carries about a one-in-two chance of the account halving and half Kelly cuts ruin risk from about 20% to 1.5% in a standard set-up ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kelly-criterion/)), while the textbook continuous-time formula puts the chance of halving at quarter Kelly near 0.8%.

A worked example makes the layers concrete. On a ₹25 lakh active book, a ₹1,000 stock with ATR of ₹25 and a 2-ATR stop would get 200 shares (₹2 lakh, 8% of the book) from a 0.4% risk budget of ₹10,000, but the gap cap limits the position to 125 shares (₹1.25 lakh). At 125 shares, risk at the stop is ₹6,250 and a 25% overnight gap costs ₹31,250, or 1.25% of the book.

### A hard-coded limit stack with a tested kill switch

Knight Capital's losses came from the absence of two controls: no check comparing orders sent with orders filled, and no capital threshold that halted trading ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)). The agent's limits live in version-controlled configuration and are enforced by pure functions outside any ML or LLM component. These are judgment-based defaults synthesised from pod and regulator practice, not evidence-derived optima:

| Layer | Control | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Order | Instrument whitelist; no naked short options; no ASM/GSM names; no synthetic stablecoins | — |
| Order | Price collar versus last traded price | 0.5–1% for equity, 1–2% for crypto |
| Order | Order type | Limit orders, or market orders with non-zero market protection |
| Trade | Risk at stop | 0.25–0.5% of active book; 1% hard maximum |
| Trade | Single-stock position cap | 5% of active book |
| Sleeve | Daily loss | 1% of active book → sleeve off for the day |
| Sleeve | Drawdown from sleeve high | −7.5% → halve; −15% → off and back to paper trading |
| Portfolio | Daily loss | 2% of active book → flatten intraday positions, no new entries |
| Portfolio | Drawdown from active-book high | −10% → halve everything; −20% → stop everything and require human review |
| Exposure | Leverage | Cash equity ≤1×; F&O gross ≤2× (the Category III AIF ceiling) with ≥50% margin headroom; crypto ≤1× (2× hard cap) |
| Exposure | Market exposure | Net beta-weighted exposure ≤0.5× active book; positions correlated above 0.7 count as one |
| Venue | Concentration | At most half the crypto sleeve on any one exchange; weekly profit sweeps to the bank |
| Rate | Order rate | Internal cap of about 2 orders a second, far below SEBI's 10 |
| Rate | Order sanity | Maximum orders per day, duplicate detection, orders-sent versus fills reconciliation |
| Kill switch | One command cancels all orders and flattens | Fires on manual command, heartbeat loss, stale data, a limit breach or a reconciliation mismatch; fire drill monthly |
| Events | Blackout | No new short-premium positions and no new swing entries before elections, the Budget, RBI policy, company results, or FOMC/CPI for crypto |

The exchange can kill the agent's algo ID under SEBI's framework ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-introduces-new-regulations-for-retail-algo-trading-to-enhance-market-safety)), so the agent must assume it can be cut off mid-position and keep protective orders resting at the exchange.

### Regime gates switch sleeves on and off, not forecasts

Volatility regimes are the most robust thing to detect: an adaptively estimated two-state hidden Markov model beat static rebalancing out of sample after costs, even with realistic signal delay ([DTU](https://www2.imm.dtu.dk/pubdb/pubs/6808-full.html)). Trend-versus-range indicators such as ADX, the Hurst exponent and variance ratios have little rigorous out-of-sample evidence, and an Indian practitioner test of a 200-day-EMA cash switch for momentum reported "significantly lower maximum drawdown" with no numbers retrievable ([Momo India](https://momoindia.substack.com/p/backtest-reducing-momentum-drawdowns)). The gates therefore stay simple and are set from rolling percentiles of each series' own history rather than fixed numbers, with every threshold tried counted as a trial in the deflated Sharpe calculation:

| Gate | Signal | Action when red |
|---|---|---|
| Volatility | India VIX percentile (practitioner bands: below 13 calm, 13–20 normal, above 20–25 stress); BTC 30-day realised-volatility percentile | Halve Sleeve A; Sleeve C stands down; Sleeve D may run, since intraday momentum is strongest on volatile days |
| Trend | Nifty versus its 200-day average; BTC versus its long average | Sleeve A to half or zero new entries; Sleeve B goes flat by its own rules |
| Momentum-crash state | Sharp market fall followed by high volatility and a rebound | Cut momentum exposure |
| Crowding | Annualised perp funding far above baseline with rising open interest | Halve crypto longs (nearly 30% funding preceded the October 2025 crash) |
| Event calendar | Scheduled binary events | Blackout as in the limit table |

A hidden Markov model, if used, must rely on filtered probabilities and act with a one-bar delay, because smoothed probabilities use future data and leak.

The simulation protocol layers four tests. A stationary block bootstrap of daily sleeve returns ([Patton, Politis & White](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Patton_Politis_White_2009.pdf)) generates at least 10,000 paths over one to three years with 5–20-day mean blocks and reports median and 95th-percentile drawdowns, time under water, and the probability of touching −10%, −20% and −30%. Trade reshuffles then repeat the exercise with the win rate cut by 5–10 points and costs doubled, and the shock days above are spliced into random points of each path. A sleeve is accepted only if its ruin probability stays below 1% and its probability of a −20% drawdown below 5% over 12 months at intended size, thresholds that are judgment rather than industry standard; otherwise its size falls in proportion to the 95th-percentile drawdown.

## Five gates separate a backtest from real capital

Allocators start new managers small, with an average first ticket of $16m against $37.7m for established managers, and grow successful positions to roughly 3.6–5 times the initial ticket ([Altss](https://altss.com/glossary/ticket-size); attribution ambiguous). The agent treats each sleeve the same way:

| Gate | Pass criteria | Minimum duration | Demotion trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Written specification | Hypothesis with an economic mechanism; universe, rules, sizing, exits and known failure regimes; trial log opened; all under version control | — | No mechanism, no gate 1 |
| 1. Backtest | Point-in-time, survivorship-free data. Full India/crypto cost stack including April 2026 STT, then **edge still positive at 2× costs**. Deflated Sharpe ≥ 0.95 ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)) or a ≥50% Sharpe haircut. Probability of backtest overfitting below 0.1–0.2 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). t-statistic above 3 ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)). Leakage smoke tests and bootstrap acceptance passed | — | Edge vanishes at 2× costs |
| 2. Walk-forward and holdout | Only concatenated out-of-sample results reported. Purged, embargoed folds ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation)). Single-use 12–24-month holdout | — | Holdout falls outside the backtest confidence band |
| 3. Paper trading on live data through the real broker API | Fills, rejects, slippage, session and static-IP plumbing verified; realised cost within 1.5× the model | 4–8 weeks and ≥100 trades | Any control failure; cost above 1.5× model |
| 4. Small live | 10–25% of target size | 6–12 months | Below the 5th percentile of the simulated distribution; drawdown above 1.5–2× the backtest maximum |
| 5. Scale | 2× steps toward full size, each after minimum-track-record evidence and slippage within the model | Typically reaches full size in years 2–3 | Any sleeve stop sends it back to gate 3 |

Gate 3 tests plumbing, not edge, because a hundred trades cannot distinguish 55% from 50%. Full scale arrives slowly because statistical proof is slow, with a Sharpe-1 sleeve needing about three years to show significance, so the scaling schedule is deliberately gradual and evidence-weighted rather than a single go/no-go verdict. The cost of that patience is low because the barbell bounds the downside throughout.

## Regulation, tax and plumbing dictate the architecture

### SEBI's algo framework and the 2024–26 F&O reset

SEBI's retail algo circular of 4 February 2025 became fully mandatory on 1 April 2026 ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-extends-deadline-for-retail-algo-trading-framework/)). Orders must come from a broker-registered app on a whitelisted **static IP** with primary and backup slots ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-introduces-new-regulations-for-retail-algo-trading-to-enhance-market-safety)); sessions use OAuth with mandatory two-factor authentication and expire daily ([Tradejini](https://www.tradejini.com/blogs/what-sebis-new-algo-trading-rules-mean-for-you)); every order carries an algo ID; strategy registration is needed only above **10 orders per second per exchange per client** ([Probe42](https://resources.probe42.in/regulatory-updates/sebi-updates/retail-algo-trading-guidelines-2025/)); API market orders must carry non-zero market protection, which also affects SL-M orders ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/reyazk08/sebis-new-algo-trading-rules-for-retail-traders-what-changed-in-april-2026-4k04)); and self-built algos may be used for oneself and immediate family below the threshold ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-extends-retail-algo-trading-framework-rollout-to-2026-125093000956_1.html)). The agent therefore needs a cloud VM with a static IP and a TOTP-assisted morning login, throttles itself far below 10 orders a second, and prefers limit orders.

### Sharing picks makes you a research analyst

Personal use of the screener needs no registration, but publishing its buy suggestions to others through a Telegram channel, an app or a website with live prices falls into research-analyst territory under SEBI's December 2024 RA amendments. Those bring a fee cap of ₹1.51 lakh per family a year ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-research-analysts-third-amendment-regulations-2024.html)), deposits of ₹1–10 lakh depending on client count ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/disclosing-ai-usage-in-investment-advisory-and-ensuring-data-security-is-a-must-sebi-issues-new-rules-for-ras-and-ias)), and mandatory disclosure of AI tool use from 30 April 2025 ([Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-mandates-ias-and-ras-to-disclose-use-of-ai-tools-in-providing-investment-advice-research-services-to-clients)). Offering the agent to others as an opaque "black-box" algo also requires RA registration and per-strategy research reports ([AlgoBulls](https://algobulls.com/blog/industry-insights-and-updates/sebi-new-algotrading-regulations-for-retail-investors)); regulated entities may not associate with unregistered people who make return claims ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/sebi-directs-regulated-entities-to-sever-ties-with-finfluencers-in-3-months)); and educational content must use price data at least three months old ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html)). Crypto signals fall outside SEBI's securities remit, though the user's tax and anti-money-laundering obligations remain.

### A seven-layer agent with a deterministic veto

The evidence against letting a language model trade is direct. In Alpha Arena's real-money crypto perpetuals season, Qwen3 Max won +22.3% while Claude Sonnet 4.5 lost 30.8%, Grok 4 lost 45.3%, Gemini 2.5 Pro lost 56.7% and GPT-5 lost 62.7% ([iWeaver](https://iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results)), and in a later US-stock season only 6 of 32 model-contest results finished in profit ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/ai-bots-auditioning-for-wall-street-trading-are-mostly-losing-money-126050701793_1.html)). The architecture therefore keeps every ML and LLM component off the order path:

Language models still earn bounded roles: summarising news into features that can only shrink or veto a trade, writing post-trade explanations, and assisting research whose outputs pass the same deflated-Sharpe and overfitting gates as any other idea.

## Conclusion

The research points to an uncomfortable inversion: for a retail agent, the hard engineering problem is not finding alpha but measuring it honestly and surviving long enough to learn whether it exists. The structure does most of the work, with the barbell, the pod stops and the defined-risk rule shrinking the worst year to a rounding error on total wealth, and the immutable log and gate process turning "is this working?" into a question with a statistical answer. The signals contribute a few percentage points at best, and those points decay, so the useful mindset is that of an allocator running a fund-of-one whose first deliverable is a trustworthy measurement system and whose second is returns.

India's 2024–26 rule changes have also quietly chosen the strategy for the trader. Higher STT on derivatives, one weekly expiry per exchange, ₹15 lakh contracts, upfront option premiums, separate tax baskets for speculative and F&O income, and a crypto regime that taxes every winner and ignores every loser all push edge away from fast, high-turnover trading and toward slower, fewer, larger-conviction positions. Because a Sharpe-1 edge needs about three years to prove, the rational plan is to start the core immediately, run every sleeve through the gates at small size, and judge the whole project in year three against the simplest alternatives an allocator would otherwise buy: an arbitrage fund or a long-short SIF.

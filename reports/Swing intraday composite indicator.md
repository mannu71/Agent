# Rank Slowly, Enter Quietly, Distrust Every Backtest

No family of technical indicators reliably tells you when an Indian stock will go up or down, and a new indicator nobody else uses is not an edge just because it is new. After survivorship correction, multiple-testing correction and realistic costs, only a handful of effects survive. The strongest are **6–12-month price momentum and closeness to the 52-week high**, which are well replicated globally and in Indian data from 1994 to 2023. Behind them come **volatility scaling of momentum exposure** (Indian peer-reviewed support from 2022), **short-term reversal used only to time entries**, and the **overnight-versus-intraday split of returns**. That split argues for buying swing positions near the close rather than at the open. Everything else is decayed, cost-eaten or untested: moving-average and RSI rules, candlesticks, Supertrend, squeezes, opening-range breakouts, VWAP crosses, order flow at retail speed, and most Indian machine-learning papers. The trading-agent repo's own real-NSE run makes this concrete. Its momentum screener has a **rank IC of only 0.014 at 5 days and 0.066 at 60 days, its top-20 picks do no better than an average stock, and the A1 breakout sleeve lost −0.22R per trade over 1,939 trades from 2017 to 2026** ([RESULTS.md](/home/user/Agent/trading-agent/RESULTS.md)). The best design the evidence supports is therefore a "Two-Speed Score" (TSS). A slow layer picks momentum leaders each week. A fast daily layer waits for a quiet, low-volume pullback in one of them. An intraday layer enters only in the last half hour, vetoes entries on crash-like or news-heavy sessions, and sizes positions down when momentum itself turns volatile. Honest expectations are **about 51–53% directional accuracy, net expectancy of 0 to +0.15R per trade, and a real chance of failing the repo's gate**. It should get zero capital until it beats both a slow-only version of itself and a Nifty200 Momentum 30 index fund in walk-forward tests and in paper trading.

## Only slow momentum clears every hurdle in India

Three hurdles kill most trading ideas, and the survey applied all three to every family. The first is replication: in the largest replication study, **65% of 452 published anomalies failed a single test and 82% failed a multiple-testing threshold**. In the "trading frictions" category, which includes short-term reversal, **102 of 106 failed**, while momentum, value, investment and profitability replicated well ([Hou, Xue & Zhang via RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i5p2019-2133..html)). The second is decay after publication: across 97 predictors, returns were **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication**, with the biggest drop three to four years after the paper appeared ([McLean & Pontiff PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)). The third is cost. Strategies whose one-sided monthly turnover stays below roughly 50% usually keep a net profit, few faster ones do, and holding on to names you would no longer buy (a "buy/hold band") is the single best way to cut cost ([Novy-Marx & Velikov, NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20721)).

Three patterns run through the table. **Everything that survives is slow, has an economic story, and is used to rank or size positions rather than to trigger trades.** Momentum persists because investors underreact and because it carries crash risk. The overnight effect has a flow explanation. Vol scaling works because momentum crashes are predictable from recent turbulence. **Everything that fails is fast or arbitrary.** Indicator triggers multiply turnover until Indian costs swallow the edge; studies across tens of thousands of rules show the best of a large universe is usually luck ([Hsu, Taylor & Wang](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/technical-trading-is-it-still-beating-the-foreign-exchange-market/)). **And no published test covers the combination this report proposes.** Each leg has its own evidence, but the composite itself is untested anywhere, India included.

LLM news signals deserve a separate warning, because they are the newest thing an "indicator no one else uses" might reach for. When LLM stock pickers were tested after their training cutoff, a model with **+20.73% annual alpha in-sample fell to −1.04%**, consistent with memorisation rather than skill ([Look-Ahead-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13770)).

## Your own data already shows how small the edge is

The repo has already run the slow half of this idea on real data, and the result should set expectations. Using NSE bhavcopy from 2016 to 2026 (3,992 symbols, delisted names included), the A1 momentum score ranked the broad universe with a mean **rank IC of 0.014 at 5 days, 0.035 at 20 days and 0.066 at 60 days** (overlap-adjusted t of 2.1, 2.6 and 3.1). That is real but weak skill, and it rises with horizon exactly as the momentum literature predicts. Yet the **top 20 picks beat the base rate by only −0.6 to +0.1 percentage points**, and IC was near zero in 2024–2025 ([RESULTS.md](/home/user/Agent/trading-agent/RESULTS.md)). The breakout trading rules built on that score lost money: **1,939 trades, a 29.7% win rate, −0.22R per trade, t = −5.8**, negative in every year except 2020. Every variant (ATR trail, no partial exit, 12-1 momentum, wider stop) also lost, and gate 1 failed on 15 logged trials. The live paper account was down 11.8% by 5 October 2026 and had been halved by its own kill rule (same source).

Two lessons follow. First, the information in momentum is spread thinly across the whole ranking, not concentrated in the top few names. A design that buys "the five best charts" throws most of it away, while one that holds many leaders and keeps them until they fall well down the ranking uses it. Second, buying breakouts at a buy-stop fights the short-horizon reversal Indian stocks show. It pays up on exactly the days when a pullback is more likely next. The TSS below inverts that entry logic: it buys leaders when they are temporarily *weak and quiet*, not when they are strong and loud.

Honest accuracy follows from the IC. Under a standard normal approximation, the chance that a stock ranked above median by the score actually beats the median is 0.5 + arcsin(IC)/π. That gives **about 51.1% at the 20-day IC of 0.035 and 52.1% at the 60-day IC of 0.066** (author's arithmetic). The best published deep-learning chart model manages just over 53% at a one-month horizon ([Jiang, Kelly & Xiu](https://www.aidf.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Xiu-Re-Imagining-Price-Trends.pdf)), and practitioners call an IC of 0.03 "genuinely good" in liquid large caps ([FE Training](https://www.fe.training/?p=10077911)). Any backtest of this composite that shows 60%+ daily accuracy is far more likely to contain look-ahead or leakage than a discovery.

Novelty does not change this arithmetic. RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, Supertrend and Ichimoku are all transforms of the same open-high-low-close-volume series. Most reduce to "how far is price from a recent average, scaled by volatility", or its reverse. A new formula is one more draw from that same universe of rules. It adds to the multiple-testing count rather than escaping it, and the best rule of a large universe tends to fail out of sample ([Bajgrowicz & Scaillet via CFA Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/02/technical-trading-revisited-false-discoveries-persistence-tests-and-transaction-costs-digest)). Only a new *input* (different data) or a structural reason (a risk premium or a behavioural friction) creates an edge. Secrecy does not protect one for long either: practitioners argue that cheap AI tools now industrialise copyable indicators faster than ever ([IBKR Quant News](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/llms-and-the-shortening-shelf-life-of-copyable-alpha/)). What *can* be distinctive is the discipline: few parameters, a slow core, cost-aware execution and ruthless validation.

## The Two-Speed Score: leaders chosen weekly, bought on quiet dips near the close

The TSS has three layers running on two clocks. Each layer carries the evidence grade from the survey and does only what that grade allows. The **slow layer** (weekly) decides *what* is worth owning: momentum leaders. The **fast layer** (daily, after the close) decides *when* a leader is worth buying: a quiet pullback. The **intraday layer** (on the entry day) decides *whether and how* to execute: in the last half hour, unless the session looks like a crash or news. A **risk layer** decides *how much* to buy. The indicator outputs, for every eligible stock and day, a slow score, a pullback reading, a state (WATCH, BUY-CANDIDATE, BUY, HOLD, SELL) and a share quantity.

### Universe and data

The universe follows the A1 rulebook, which is already coded. It includes NSE EQ-series stocks with a close of at least ₹100, average 20-day traded value of at least ₹10 crore, and at least 260 daily bars. It excludes ASM/GSM/ESM surveillance names, trade-for-trade series, SME stocks and 2%/5% price-band stocks. It is rebuilt point-in-time from bhavcopy including delisted names, because survivor-only Indian small-cap backtests overstate returns by about 4.94 percentage points a year ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19380)). Inputs are corporate-action-adjusted daily OHLCV, the Nifty 50 and India VIX for the existing regime gate, the results calendar, and 5-minute bars for the intraday layer. Notation: C is the daily close, H the daily high and V the daily volume. t is the latest completed session. σ is the annualised standard deviation of daily log returns over 252 sessions (the repo's `ann_vol(s, i, 252)`), and σ_d = σ/√252 is the daily equivalent. z(·) is the repo's cross-sectional z-score, clipped at ±3 (`zscore_clipped`).

### Layer 1, slow: who is a leader (grade A)

```
M6  = (C[t-21] / C[t-126] - 1) / σ          # 6-month return, skipping the latest month, per unit of risk
M12 = (C[t-21] / C[t-252] - 1) / σ          # 12-month return, same skip
P52 = C[t] / max(H[t-251 .. t])             # closeness to the 52-week high (1.0 = at the high)
S   = ( z(M6) + z(M12) + z(P52) ) / 3       # equal weights, z across the whole universe each day
q_S = percentile rank of S within the universe (0 = worst, 1 = best)
LEADER (enter) if q_S ≥ 0.80 ;  stays LEADER (hold) while q_S ≥ 0.60
```

The return-divided-by-volatility form is NSE's own Normalised Momentum Score method ([HDFC MF](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf)). P52 carries Raju's more stable Indian alpha. The 6-month leg reflects the IIT-Bombay finding that 6-month formation gave the best risk-adjusted long-only Indian results ([Nigam & Pandey](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3233/AF-220399)). The one-month skip is a deliberate design choice (U). It keeps the slow score from rewarding exactly the recent run-up that Indian one-month reversal tends to undo, and it leaves the last month to the fast layer, so the two layers do not double-count. Equal weights are not laziness: estimated "optimal" weights usually do worse out of sample (the "forecast combination puzzle", [VU Amsterdam](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/the-forecast-combination-puzzle-a-simple-theoretical-explanation-2/)). The 0.80 / 0.60 gap is a buy/hold band, the most effective cost control in the Novy-Marx–Velikov study. The leader set is recomputed every session but acted on for exits only at the weekly review (last session of the week), which keeps turnover low.

### Layer 2, fast: when a leader is worth buying (grade B / B−)

```
r5   = C[t] / C[t-5] - 1
r5*  = r5 - median over universe of r5             # market-neutral; sector-neutral once sector data exists
PB   = - r5* / (σ_d × √5)                          # pullback size in 5-day standard deviations (higher = deeper dip)
RV5  = SMA5(V) / SMA50(V)                          # recent volume vs normal
BUY-CANDIDATE if  LEADER(enter)  and  PB ≥ 1.0  and  RV5 ≤ 1.0
```

In plain words: a stock that is a long-term leader but has fallen at least one "normal week's move" more than the market over the last five days, on below-normal volume, becomes a candidate. The reversal evidence says such dips in Indian stocks tend to bounce, especially the quiet ones ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/rbfpps/rbf-06-2018-0058.html)). The momentum evidence says the leader should keep drifting up over months. Low volume matters for a second reason: a pullback on heavy volume often means new information, and high-volume moves behave differently ([Lee & Swaminathan](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v55y2000i5p2017-2069.html)). Subtracting the market median is a first step toward the neutralised form that survives costs. Robeco found that industry/factor neutralisation is what makes reversal work ([Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2023/10/reversing-the-trend-of-short-term-reversal)), so a sector-relative r5* should replace the market median once the engine has sector data. A candidate stays live for at most **3 sessions**, and only while it remains a leader with PB ≥ 0.5. When there are more candidates than free slots, they are ranked by S, the better-evidenced slow score, not by the size of the dip.

### Layer 3, intraday: whether and how to execute on the entry day (grade B for timing, C for vetoes)

On the session after a stock becomes a candidate, the indicator reads 5-minute bars, using only bars that have *finished* by 15:00:

```
d      = P_1500 / C[t] - 1                          # today's move into 15:00 (P_1500 = close of the bar ending 15:00)
RVOL_d = Σ V(09:15–15:00 today) / median over prior 20 sessions of Σ V(09:15–15:00)
BUY at 15:00–15:25 if  d > -2 × σ_d   and  RVOL_d ≤ 1.5   and  regime allows new entries
order: limit at P_1500 × 1.002, cancel if unfilled by 15:25 (candidate carries over, within its 3-session life)
```

The timing has the strongest support in this layer. Momentum profits accrue overnight, not during the day ([Lou, Polk & Skouras](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481)), and the Sensex's intraday leg lost nearly everything over 24 years while its overnight leg compounded ([Knuteson](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.00223)). Buying in the last half hour instead of at 09:15 therefore keeps the part of the day that historically paid and skips the part that did not. It also avoids the opening spike in spreads and volatility: Nifty intraday volatility is U-shaped, highest at the open ([MPRA](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/89689/)). The two vetoes are risk controls with weaker support (grade C, every threshold U). A fall of more than two daily standard deviations into 15:00, or volume running at 1.5× normal, signals that the "quiet pullback" has become a news event, so the indicator stands aside. The market-level veto reuses the repo's existing regime gate rather than inventing a new one. That gate blocks entries when the Nifty is below its 200-day average, the crash state is on, or an event blackout applies. The intraday layer **never opens and closes a position on the same day**. At about 0.08% statutory cost plus slippage per intraday round trip, and with 71% of SEBI-studied intraday traders losing, it exists only to make one delivery purchase cheaper and better timed.

### Selling: five exits, in priority order

```
1. Protective stop    : SL-limit resting at the exchange at E - 3 × ATR20(at entry)     (R = 3 × ATR20)
2. Leader exit        : at the weekly review, if q_S < 0.60 → sell next session, 15:00–15:25
3. Results rule       : at the close before a scheduled results board meeting, sell half (rest keeps its stop)
4. Regime de-risking  : if m_reg × m_vol falls below 50% of the position's sizing value → trim to the new size
5. Failed pullback    : if after 10 sessions the close is still below entry and PB ≥ 1.0 again → sell
```

The stop is wide because the entry is a dip-buy. Stops add value only when returns trend, and under a random walk they always lower expected return ([Kaminski & Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)), so a tight stop on a mean-reversion entry would mostly harvest noise. A stop on momentum positions is the one place stops have strong support: a 10% stop cut momentum's worst month from −49.79% to −11.34%, though the robustness of that result is questioned ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/08/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/)). The main exit is not a price target. It is the stock leaving the leader band, because the 60-day IC is the strongest number the repo has measured and profits in stock trends come from a small minority of long holds. Exit 5 is the "vertical barrier" of triple-barrier labelling ([Hudson Thames](https://hudsonthames.org/does-meta-labeling-add-to-signal-efficacy/)): a dip that keeps dipping for two weeks has failed its thesis. Exits 3–5 are design rules (U) and count as trials.

### Sizing: risk-based, cut when momentum itself is turbulent (grade A−)

```
LB      = daily equal-weight return of the current leader set (q_S ≥ 0.80)
m_vol   = min(1, median over prior 756 sessions of σ126(LB) / σ126(LB))
m_reg   = existing EquityRegime multiplier (1, 0.5 or 0 from the 200-DMA and India-VIX gates)
shares  = floor( m_reg × m_vol × min( 0.004·E_A / (3·ATR20),  0.05·E_A / P,  0.01·ADV20 / P ) )
skip if 0.005·P / (3·ATR20) > 0.25        # cost gate: 0.5% round trip must stay under 0.25R
at most 15 positions, 3 per sector, shared 3% open-risk cap with any other long-momentum sleeve
```

m_vol is the Barroso–Santa-Clara idea applied to the leader basket itself. It is preferred to an on/off switch because the 2024–25 Indian momentum drawdown (−31.79% while the Nifty 50 fell 15.35%) was a rotation, not a market crash, so index-based gates would have missed it ([HDFC MF](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf)). Volatility targeting also raises Sharpe ratios in equities and reduces extreme outcomes in every asset class tested ([Quantpedia on Man Group](https://quantpedia.com/the-impact-of-volatility-targeting-on-equities-bonds-commodities-and-currencies)). The fancier volatility-managed alphas, however, fail out of sample and after costs ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2018/12/17/dont-ride-out-the-storm-why-you-can-and-should-time-volatility)), so m_vol only ever reduces size and is rebalanced only when it moves by more than 50%. The 0.40% risk, 5% cap, 1%-of-ADV cap and 0.25R cost gate are the repo's existing rulebook values. The cost gate means the indicator rejects any stock whose ATR20 is under about 0.67% of price, because a 0.5% delivery round trip would then eat too much of R.

| Intraday cut-off / window | 15:00 / 15:00–15:25 | Overnight-return evidence | Times U |
| Intraday vetoes | d > −2σ_d; RVOL_d ≤ 1.5 | Risk logic only | U |
| Stop | 3 × ATR20 | Kaminski-Lo; Han-Zhou-Zhu | U |
| Failed-pullback time stop | 10 sessions | Triple-barrier logic | U |
| m_vol | σ126 of leaders vs 756-day median | Barroso-SC; Singh et al. (India) | Form V; windows U |

**Every U value is a backtest trial**, and only two of them (PB threshold and stop multiple) should be swept at first. Everything else is frozen at the defaults above before any data is looked at.

## Expect 51–53% and prove it in seven steps before real money

The validation gate is the repo's existing gate 1 with two TSS-specific additions. The existing thresholds are: net expectancy ≥ +0.15R at 1× costs (0.5% round trip) and > 0 at 2× costs; deflated Sharpe ≥ 0.95 across every logged trial; PBO < 0.20; t > 3; maximum drawdown ≤ 25%; no year above 40% of P&L; and still positive without the top 1% of trades ([validate.hpp](/home/user/Agent/trading-agent/include/ta/validate.hpp)). The first addition: **TSS must beat its own slow-only baseline** (the same leaders, bought on the weekly review at the close with no pullback or intraday layer) by at least +0.05R per trade with t ≥ 2. Otherwise the fast and intraday layers are complexity without benefit and get removed. The second: **after tax, it must beat simply holding a Nifty200 Momentum 30 index fund**, which is the blueprint's fallback when a momentum sleeve fails. One-sided monthly turnover should also stay below 50%, the line below which most anomalies keep a net profit.

The most important honesty point concerns the holdout. The repo has already examined the slow score on 2017–2026 data, so **no truly untouched historical holdout remains for the slow layer**. The only clean out-of-sample test left is the future, which is why step 7 is not optional. Sample size is the other constraint. With a per-trade standard deviation around 1.5R, reaching t = 3 needs (3 × 1.5 / μ)² trades: **about 900 trades if the true edge is +0.15R and about 2,025 at +0.10R** (the rulebook's arithmetic). At 15 positions turning over a few times a year, live data alone cannot prove a small edge for years. The backtest has to carry most of the statistical weight, and live trading mainly checks that nothing broke.

What the user should expect if everything passes is modest. The hit rate on individual trades will probably land near 45–55%, with winners somewhat larger than losers. Ranking accuracy against the median stock should be about 51–53%. Net expectancy should be 0 to +0.15R per trade before tax, with long losing stretches, because a 15-name book has little breadth. Drawdowns will cluster with momentum crashes, which in India have reached −62% for the raw factor. m_vol should soften them but cannot remove them. The base case should explicitly include failure. A1 used the same slow inputs and failed every variant. TSS changes the entry logic, holding period and sizing for evidence-based reasons, but none of those changes has been tested. If TSS fails at step 2 or 3, the evidence-backed action is to put the money in the index fund and stop, not to add a sixth indicator.

## Conclusion

The search for an indicator that "tells you when stocks go up and down" ends somewhere less exciting and more useful. The robust information in Indian prices is slow (months, not minutes) and spread thinly across hundreds of stocks. The faster tools that feel predictive are mostly ways to pay costs. That turns the design problem around: the job of the fast and intraday layers is not to predict, but to **buy the slow edge more cheaply and size it more safely**. They buy leaders on quiet dips rather than loud breakouts, near the close rather than at the open, and smaller when momentum itself is turbulent. If the TSS adds anything, it will be through those execution choices, and the slow-only baseline in step 2 measures exactly that.

The repo's own failed A1 run is the most valuable piece of evidence here. It is a correctly measured negative result, on real data, from a system that looked reasonable on paper. Every number in this report came from search summaries of blocked full texts, so decimals such as Raju's 20.39%, the Indian PEAD findings and Singh et al.'s Sharpe doubling need checking before they are relied on. But the direction of the evidence is consistent. Treat the TSS as a pre-registered hypothesis, with an index fund as the default answer and a written condition that would prove it wrong.

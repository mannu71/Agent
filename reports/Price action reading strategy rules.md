# Code the Skeleton, Not the Trader's Eye

"The Power of Price Action Reading" (Zarattini & Stamatoudis, SSRN 4879527) does not contain a rulebook you can trade. Its own mechanical rules for buying 9,794 US overnight gap-ups from 2016 to 2023 lost money in every variant tested. The headline result (**3,968% total return, 59.1% CAGR, Sharpe 1.70, 35% maximum drawdown**) appeared only after one champion trader looked at anonymised charts, approved 18% of them, and then managed each trade bar by bar ([Concretum Substack](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/the-power-of-price-action-reading); [Paper PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/The-Power-Of-Price-Action-Reading.pdf)). An investor should read the paper as a measurement of what a skilled human adds: about **+0.25R per trade from chart selection alone** and another +0.3–0.55R from discretionary management. It is not evidence that code can capture either. The result comes from one trader, has no out-of-sample test and no independent replication, and its cost and survivorship assumptions are unconfirmed. The PDF itself could not be read during this research, so every parameter below that comes from it needs checking against its tables before anyone codes it. Two parts can be coded. The first is a narrow mechanical skeleton: a gap of 6% or more, a positive first 5-minute bar, a buy-stop at that bar's high, a stop below its low, a 10-day moving-average trail and a 30-day cap. The second is a set of numeric proxies for the three chart traits the trader preferred. On Indian delivery costs of about 0.5% per round trip, the skeleton's best case is roughly break-even. The related Concretum papers are fully specified, but they split sharply. The intraday ones earn a few basis points per trade, which Indian costs exceed (one NSE opening-range test lost 21.8% out of sample). The daily crypto Donchian ensemble (BTC net Sharpe about 1.56 with a 19% drawdown) is the most cost-robust and transfers best. The rulebook below therefore keeps a precisely specified momentum-breakout swing sleeve and the crypto trend sleeve live at small size. It holds the episodic-pivot gap sleeve and every intraday rule at zero capital until each passes an India-specific backtest that first reproduces the paper's losing baseline. The earlier blueprint's barbell (75% core, 25% active book) stays intact. No rulebook makes trading foolproof: every rule here will have losing trades, losing months and possibly losing years, and its job is only to keep losses bounded, measurable and easy to switch off.

## Selection, not the gap, produced the 3,968%

### The paper's six stages show the edge entering only with the human

The study took every US stock that gapped up about 6% or more overnight between 2016 and 2023. That gave **9,794 events with an average gap of about 28%**, which means a sample dominated by volatile small and mid caps. The SSRN keywords ("overnight gaps, gap-go, PEAD, earnings gaps") point to earnings as the main catalyst ([Paper PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/The-Power-Of-Price-Action-Reading.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4879527)). On average these stocks "drift downward gradually" after the gap. The authors then tested progressively smarter mechanical rules before handing the dataset to Marios Stamatoudis, a verified **+291%** top-five finisher in the 2023 US Investing Championship ([TraderLion](https://traderlion.com/investing-champions/marios-stamatoudis-usic/)). In this report, **V** marks a detail that appears in a source quoting the paper or its authors, and **U** marks one that is unverified or inferred.

| Stage | Rule | Reported outcome | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open, no stop | Buy every gap at the gap-day open | "Significant negative edge" | V |
| Open, 1-ATR stop | Same, with a stop 1 ATR below entry | Better, "but still losses after 8 days" | V (ATR length unknown) |
| Pos OR | Trade only if the first 5-minute bar closes above its open; buy-stop at that bar's high; stop 1 cent below its low; hold up to 30 days | Improved, "continues to be unprofitable" | V |
| Pos OR + Trailing | Same, plus a 10-day simple-moving-average trailing stop | Slightly better, "not sufficiently to make it profitable" | V (close-based or touch-based trail unknown) |
| + 4 Targets + Trader | Trader approves 1,721 of 9,794 gaps (about 18%) from anonymised daily charts; position split into four 25% partial exits | Average trade peaks at **+0.25R on day 12** | V (target levels unknown) |
| Micromanaged | Trader manages entries, partials and exits bar by bar without seeing future bars | **+0.55R on the gap day, +0.80R by day 4**; $100k grows to more than $4M | V (stage attribution probable, not certain) |

Sources: [Paper PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/The-Power-Of-Price-Action-Reading.pdf); [Concretum Substack](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/the-power-of-price-action-reading); [Stamatoudis on X](https://x.com/stamatoudism/status/1795787641490698254).

The review was set up to remove obvious bias. Charts carried no ticker, date, price scale, news or market context, only about two years of daily history and the gap itself, and the trader replayed each trade without seeing future bars ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/combining-discretionary-and-algorithmic-trading/)). The authors report three traits he preferred: **gaps after a period of neglect, gaps that break multi-week or multi-month ranges, and gaps early in a momentum cycle rather than late** ([Concretum Substack](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/the-power-of-price-action-reading)). The paper does not turn any of these into formulas. The headline figures are internally consistent. On my arithmetic, about 215 trades a year at 1% risk and +0.5–0.8R each gives roughly 100–170R a year before compounding, which fits a CAGR in the 50s once overlapping positions and leverage caps are allowed for. The portfolio's sizing convention (1% risk per trade, 4× leverage cap, $0.0005-per-share commission, no slippage) is Concretum's house style from its opening-range papers. It is not confirmed for this paper.

### An allocator should treat it as a hypothesis generator, not a track record

Each weakness alone would justify a heavy discount, and they compound. The evidence rests on one trader, and Concretum has itself published on how randomness alone produces apparent "market wizards" ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/the-illusion-of-exceptional-performance-how-randomness-can-create-market-wizards/)). The authors also hedge their conclusion: discretion helps "at least when conducted by a skilled trader" ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4879527)). Anonymisation cannot rule out recognition. With a 28% average gap in the 2020–21 retail mania, a trader can recognise famous runners from their shape. The robustness check removed only the **five best trades**, about 0.3% of 1,721 ([Stamatoudis on X](https://x.com/stamatoudism/status/1795787641490698254)), which is a weak test for a right-skewed strategy. The paper reports no win rate, profit factor or payoff ratio, no out-of-sample period, no confirmed slippage on small-cap gappers, and no confirmation that delisted stocks were included. No independent replication or published critique was found. Everything I could find is supportive commentary that adds no data ([Retail Traders Repository](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/trading-research-paper-the-power-of-price-action-reading); [QuantSeeker](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/popular-investing-research-in-2024)).

On the evidence available, I grade the paper **D as evidence for a systematic strategy and B as a hypothesis generator**. Its most useful finding is negative and probably robust: buying large gaps mechanically loses money. Its positive finding marks where the edge lives, in selecting which gaps to trade and in managing the first few days. That gives an agent one precise thing to test, which is whether coded proxies for "neglect, range breakout, early cycle" reproduce any part of the +0.25R selection uplift. The extra +0.3–0.55R from bar-by-bar management should be assumed unattainable by code and left out of every expectation.

## Concretum's codeable siblings carry thin, cost-fragile edges

### Seven papers, one template, two very different robustness profiles

The rule-based Concretum papers can be fully coded from public sources, and independent replicators have matched several of them closely. They share a template: 14-day lookbacks, 1% risk per trade or a volatility target, a 4× intraday leverage cap (200% for daily strategies), and cost models that charge commission but little or no slippage. That consistency limits the authors' freedom to tune parameters, but none of the parameters were pre-registered.

Kakushadze and Serur's "151 Trading Strategies" is the useful contrast. It gives formulas for more than 150 strategies but no tested parameters, cost models or equity curves ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3247865)). It is a catalogue, not a source of rules.

### What the replications teach the agent's design

Three lessons carry straight into the rulebook. First, **intraday edges in this family are a few basis points or a few cents per trade**: about 7¢ a share gross on QQQ and about 2.6 bps a trade on the SPY noise area. They are concentrated in volatile years such as 2020 and 2022, and both papers' samples end before the 2025–26 weakness that replicators found. Second, **frozen published parameters beat re-optimisation**. The SPY replicator's 27-variant grid picked the paper's own configuration, and quarterly walk-forward reselection lowered Sharpe ([GitHub](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2024-momentum-spy)). The agent should therefore start from published defaults and count every deviation as a trial. Third, **the daily trend papers are the robust branch**. They charge 10 bps a trade, turn over slowly and still show Sharpe ratios near 1.5, so they survive Indian cost stacks that destroy the intraday branch. One conflict of interest belongs on the record: co-author Andrew Aziz runs a day-trading education business ([GitHub replication](https://github.com/giovannibrusco/zarattini-2023-orb-qqq)).

## Indian plumbing rewrites every gap and breakout rule

### Bands, auctions and mid-session results change what a "gap" is

A US gap rule ported directly to NSE breaks in four places.

**Price bands.** Non-F&O stocks have fixed bands capped at 20%. As of April 2023, 1,595 of 2,241 NSE stocks sat in the 20% band, 295 in the 5% band and 151 in the 10% band ([Chittorgarh](https://www.chittorgarh.com/report/stock-nse-circuit-filters/120/20/)). A big-news gap can therefore open locked at the upper circuit and be impossible to buy. F&O stocks have a dynamic 10% band. It widens in 5% steps, but only after at least 50 trades by 10 different client codes and 3 trading members at 9.90% or more of the base price, followed by a **15-minute cooling-off** ([FYERS](https://fyers.in/notice-board/revised-dynamic-price-bands-and-fo-segment-rules/); [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/sebi-to-enforce-dynamic-price-bands-for-all-fo-stocks-equally-on-exchanges)).

**The opening auction.** The 09:00–09:15 pre-open call auction sets the open as an equilibrium price, so the first 5-minute bar at 09:15 starts on an auction-discovered print ([NSE](https://www.nseindia.com/products-services/equity-market-special-pre-open-session)). Current-month index and stock futures have had their own pre-open auction since 8 December 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/capital-market-news/nse-to-commence-pre-opening-session-in-f-o-segment-from-december-08-125110400728_1.html)).

**Results timing.** Results must reach the exchange within 30 minutes of the board meeting ending, so many Indian "earnings gaps" happen in the middle of the session rather than overnight ([StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/faqs-on-sebi-lodr-amendments-dated-05th-may-2021-102028.html)). The market also responds differently to announcements made during and after trading hours ([Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/AJAR-04-2019-0023/full/html)).

**Product rules.** Intraday equity leverage is capped at 5×, and cash-market shorts must be squared off the same day ([Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/margins/margin-leverage-and-product-and-order-types/articles/how-much-margins-leverage-does-zerodha-provide)). From GSM Stage II onward, intraday trading is not permitted at all ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/graded-surveillance-measure)).

Indian drift evidence is mixed, so it supports gap continuation only weakly. One study of 2002–2011 found significant post-event abnormal returns in **35 of 37 quarters** ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/vision/v19y2015i1p25-36.html)). A 2014–2018 study of 100 NSE firms found a *negative* link between abnormal returns and earnings surprise ([VUT](https://journals.lib.vutbr.cz/index.php/trends/article/view/541)). Another found prices absorb good news efficiently but drift down for more than a week after bad news ([Nottingham](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/26569)). No study measures whether NSE stocks that gap 5–10% on results continue or fade, so the agent must produce that statistic itself. Extreme intraday moves in Indian stocks also tend to reverse within minutes ([IJFM](https://publishingindia.com/archive/ijfm/prior-return-effect-in-indian-stock-market-an-intra-day-analysis)), which argues against buying the first minute of a gap.

These costs reshape each candidate, and the conversion to R is the key calculation. A gap trade with its stop below the first 5-minute bar typically risks 2–5% of price. A 0.5% round trip therefore costs **0.1–0.27R per trade** (author's arithmetic), which matches or exceeds the +0.25R the trader's selection added in US data. The Stocks-in-Play stop is 10% of ATR. On a ₹1,000 stock with a ₹20 ATR that is a ₹2 (0.2%) stop, so the 0.18% intraday cost consumes **about 0.9R per trade**. The paper's reported average for the relative-volume-filtered set is +0.08R (from a secondary source, U). That is consistent with the public NSE opening-range test, which earned ₹173 gross on 759 trades against ₹2,357 of costs and lost **21.8% over four years**, losing in every out-of-sample year ([GitHub](https://github.com/md0n-cmd/indian-equity-backtests)).

## The rulebook: parameters, sources and expected edge by sleeve

### Conventions shared by every sleeve

R is the distance from entry to initial stop. E_A is active-book equity, marked at the prior close. ADV20 is the 20-day median traded value in rupees. Every input carries an `available_at` timestamp, and any feature dated after the decision time is rejected. Protective stops rest at the exchange as stop-limit orders, because the exchange can kill the agent's algo ID mid-position. Every position is sized by one formula, applied after the regime multiplier m:

**q = floor( m × min( r_s·E_A / (P_entry − P_stop), 0.05·E_A / P_entry, 0.01·ADV20 / P_entry ) )**

Here r_s is the sleeve's risk per trade, 5% is the blueprint's single-stock cap, and 1% of ADV20 is a liquidity cap (U). The regime multiplier is m = 1 when the Nifty 50 closes above its 200-day average and India VIX sits below its one-year 80th percentile, 0.5 when one of those conditions fails, and 0 for new entries when both fail. This sharpens the blueprint's qualitative gate; the thresholds are U and count as trials. The sleeve skips a trade when q × P is below ₹25,000, because the fixed DP charge makes smaller delivery trades disproportionately costly. It also skips a trade when **modelled round-trip cost exceeds 0.25R** (the cost gate). That gate follows directly from the cost table and is the single most important India-specific rule.

### Sleeve A1: momentum-leader breakouts (live at small size)

This sleeve makes the blueprint's swing sleeve precise. It combines the evidence on 6–12-month momentum and 52-week-high proximity for selection with the published Kullamägi breakout mechanics for timing. Those mechanics are qualitative practitioner rules with a self-reported 25–30% win rate ([Breakouts Happen](https://breakoutshappen.com/stock-news/how-to-trade-like-qullamaggie-setups-strategy-and-screener)) and no rigorous backtest. The only "backtest" found studied the prior year's top 100 winners ([Financial Wisdom TV](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/qullamaggie-breakout-setup-case-study-what-the-top-100-winning-stocks-reveal)), which is pure survivorship bias.

**Expected realistic edge.** The blueprint's planning range for trend and breakout trades is a 35–45% win rate at 2–3× payoff. That implies about +0.3–0.6R gross, or roughly +0.2–0.5R after a 0.1R cost at a 5% stop (author's arithmetic, U). Two facts pull the estimate down. In a 2026 Indian backtest, the liquid half of a momentum portfolio earned only **8.51% net CAGR against 10.41% for the Nifty 50**, and all of the alpha sat in the illiquid half ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/)). Kullamägi's own reported win rate is also nearer 25%. The plan should therefore assume **0 to +0.2R per trade after costs and before tax**, and treat anything more as unproven.

### Sleeve A2: episodic-pivot gaps, the paper's skeleton adapted to NSE (paper-trade only)

This sleeve is the direct translation of SSRN 4879527. The mechanical layer is the paper's. The selection layer is a set of coded proxies for the trader's three stated preferences. **None of those proxies comes from the paper**; they are hypotheses to test.

**Expected realistic edge.** The unfiltered skeleton is negative in US data (V) and should be assumed negative in India. With a perfect replica of the trader's eye, the filtered set peaked at +0.25R before costs that the paper never stated. A coded proxy will capture some fraction of that, and Indian costs take 0.1–0.27R. The honest central estimate is **−0.2R to +0.1R per trade, roughly zero**. That is why the sleeve gets no capital until its own backtest shows otherwise.

**Expected realistic edge.** The paper reports BTC net CAGR of 30% at 17% volatility (Sharpe 1.56) and ETH at 27%/1.51. Several things discount that. The standard 50% backtest haircut takes the Sharpe to about 0.8, and funding drag on partial exposure is about 0.5–3% a year. The worst-case VDA tax then applies. Together these give roughly **5–8% a year after tax with a plan for 25–30% drawdowns** (author's arithmetic, U). That is barely above the 5.3% T-bill hurdle. The sleeve's justification is therefore diversification and protection in a slow crash, not raw return; it fares better if INR-settled perps keep business-income treatment. The paper itself has internal inconsistencies that need resolving against the PDF: its printed MAR of 0.88 does not match 30/19, and Sortino moves between the gross and net versions while Sharpe barely changes ([GitHub](https://github.com/alfred1123/Quant_Strategies/pull/61)). A crypto "episodic pivot" is not part of this sleeve. Exchange-listing pumps largely give back their gains within about two weeks ([Blockchain Research Lab](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/de/2019/09/10/market-reaction-to-exchange-listings-of-cryptocurrencies-2/)). Only the largest coins show daily continuation, and small coins reverse ([Medeniyet repository](https://earsiv.medeniyet.edu.tr/items/23a96c9f-3952-4bdf-aec5-abafb3aaab70)). Buying listing-day spikes is therefore banned, and a large-cap "daily return ≥ 2.5 × ADR on a dated catalyst" event study stays research-only.

## Human judgment is the part no code inherits

The line between codeable and discretionary runs through the middle of the paper. The agent's expectations should be built only from the codeable side.

| Element of the paper's result | Codeable? | How the agent handles it | Share of the published edge it plausibly carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gap ≥ 6%, catalyst, liquidity | Yes | Hard filters (V for 6%) | None; the unfiltered set loses |
| Positive first 5-minute bar, buy-stop at its high, stop below its low | Yes | Exact rule (V) | Turns a large loss into a smaller one |
| 10-day SMA trail, 30-day cap | Yes | Exact rule; close-based assumed | Marginal improvement, still unprofitable |
| Four 25% partials | Partly | Levels unknown, tested as a grid | Part of the +0.25R stage |
| Approval of 18% from anonymised charts | No; proxies only | Neglect, range-breakout and early-cycle scores, top 20% approved | Up to +0.25R if the proxies work, likely much less |
| Bar-by-bar management of entries, adds, partials and exits | No | Excluded from expectations | The extra +0.3–0.55R (micromanaged stage) |
| Recognition of famous charts despite anonymisation | Not legitimately | Treated as possible contamination of the published edge | Unknown, possibly material |

One consequence matters for anyone tempted to "add an LLM to read the chart". A language model trained on 2016–2023 market history is maximally exposed to the recognition problem, and LLM-derived features are look-ahead-contaminated for any period before the model's training cutoff. The blueprint's ban on LLMs in the order path also applies to chart approval. A vision or language model may at most produce a logged, out-of-sample-only score that the deterministic screener can use as one more ranked feature, and only after it passes the same gates.

## Backtests must reproduce the paper's failure before trusting any success

### One protocol, with a reproduce-first step

Every sleeve goes through the blueprint's gates, from written specification to scaling, with three additions specific to this rulebook. First, **replicate before adapting**. The agent's code must reproduce a published number on the original market before it touches Indian data: crypto ensemble trade counts and Sharpe on BTC, the SPY noise-area trade count, and the QQQ ORB trade count. Replicators reached 1,775 against 1,795 trades and 1,379 noise-area trades, so a gap of more than about 5% signals a code bug, not an edge. Second, **reproduce the negative baseline**. The Indian gap backtest must first show the paper's mechanical variants losing. If "buy every 6% NSE gap at the open" comes out strongly positive, the likely culprit is look-ahead, survivorship or fills at locked circuits. Third, **freeze defaults and count trials**. Every grid value in the tables above counts toward the deflated-Sharpe and overfitting-probability calculation, because re-optimisation degraded the SPY replication.

Sample size is the binding constraint for the gap sleeve. On my arithmetic, with a per-trade standard deviation of about 1.5R (U), a t-statistic of 3 needs n = (3 × 1.5/μ)² trades. That is **324 trades if the true edge is +0.25R, 900 at +0.15R and 2,025 at +0.10R**. If NSE produces only a few hundred qualifying catalyst gaps a year after the liquidity and band filters, a coded A2 edge near +0.1R cannot be proven in fewer than several years of combined backtest and live data.

The blueprint's architecture stands: a 75% core in T-bills, liquid and arbitrage funds, a 25% active book, a halve-at-7.5%, off-at-15% stop on each sleeve, and halve-at-10%, stop-at-20% on the whole active book. The refinements below change what sits inside the active book and add the sleeve-specific triggers this rulebook needs.

## Conclusion

The paper's lasting contribution is to show where retail edge in gap trading actually lives, which is not where most rule-sellers put it. The gap itself, the opening-range trigger and the moving-average trail are commodity mechanics that lose money by themselves. What pays is a selection and management skill that one champion could exercise on anonymised charts and that nobody has yet shown can be coded. For an India-based agent this flips the usual question. The issue is not which rule to copy but whether a measurable proxy for that judgment survives about 0.5% round-trip costs, price bands and results released mid-session. Building the approved-versus-rejected log from the first day of paper trading is therefore worth more than any parameter in the tables above.

The wider Concretum family points the same way the blueprint's regulatory analysis did. Speed-dependent intraday edges of a few basis points cannot survive India's post-2026 cost stack, while slow, volatility-sized trend rules with ratcheting stops can. The live allocation should therefore tilt toward fewer, longer, rule-exact positions, with the gap and intraday sleeves held at zero capital until their own data argues otherwise. No version of this rulebook is foolproof. The realistic aim is a set of small, bounded bets whose failure the agent will detect quickly and survive cheaply, judged in year three against an arbitrage fund and a momentum index fund.

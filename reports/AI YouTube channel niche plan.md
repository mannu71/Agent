# Build English history docs on rented Wan2GP

The best bet for a faceless, AI-voiced, no-GPU, budget creator in late 2026 is an **English-language, research-led history and "what if" mini-documentary channel**. Make **10–20 minute long-form videos weekly** and cut **2–4 Shorts** from each one. Treat Hindi as a later expansion through YouTube's auto-dubbing, not as the launch market. The reason is money per view. History earns roughly **$7–14 RPM with US-weighted audiences**, while India-audience content earns **5–10x less**, and Hindi earns **2–5x less than English** inside India. That makes a view from the global English audience worth roughly 10–30x a Hindi view. History is also one of the few AI-friendly niches that YouTube still rewards and that has not been flooded with slop.

Wan2GP (now branded WanGP) can produce the visuals, but it should be your b-roll studio, not the whole pipeline. It is free and very actively maintained (v13.141 shipped on 29 Sep 2026). It runs Wan 2.2, LTX-2.x, MiniMax H3 and strong image and TTS models. With no GPU you rent one: an RTX 4090 on RunPod costs about **$0.34/hr**, which keeps AI video at an estimated **$1–3 per finished video** plus storage. Use only models whose licenses allow monetization: Wan 2.2 and LTX-2.x for video, Apache-licensed image models, and a commercial-safe voice.

Policy is where you have to be most careful. YouTube monetizes AI-assisted work but demonetizes whole channels for templated, interchangeable "inauthentic content". It also forbids monetizing AI "expert" personas on finance, health or law. And from **1 Feb 2027**, new channels need **8,000 watch hours** (up from 4,000) to join the YouTube Partner Program (YPP). So the first 90 days should focus on a distinct, well-sourced series format, honest AI disclosure, and long-form watch time.

## History mini-docs win the risk-adjusted niche ranking

No niche is best on every measure, so the choice is a trade-off between payout, saturation, policy risk and fit with AI visuals. Finance and AI-tools content pays the most, at an estimated **$10–40 RPM** ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/how-much-do-youtubers-make/)). But it is the most crowded niche. And since July 2026, YouTube has said that an **AI persona presenting itself as a human expert on finance, legal or health topics cannot be monetized** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). A faceless newcomer with an AI voice is poorly placed to win there.

The cheapest formats are a trap. Sleep and ambient videos, horror slideshows and scary stories earn about **$0.50–2.50 RPM** and face "brutal competition" ([YouTubeNiches.com](https://youtubeniches.com/blog/best-faceless-youtube-niches-2026)). They also sit right in the category YouTube has been purging.

History lands in the sweet spot. Large faceless channels report **$7–14 RPM** for US-focused history, $6–12 for true crime and $5–9 for mythology ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/he-made-$300k-from-videos-people-fall-asleep-to-inside-youtubes-ai-slop-economy)). The topics are evergreen and match search queries ("What if Rome never fell?"). History also needs visuals that never existed on camera, which is exactly what AI images and clips supply.

| Option | Est. RPM | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|
| **1. English history / "what if" mini-docs** | $7–14 (US-weighted) | Evergreen, search-friendly, works as a series, AI visuals fit naturally. Main risk is accuracy. |
| 2. English science/space "what happens if" explainers | ~$4–12 (education band) | Proven by Zack D. Films (27.6M subs). Stylized visuals hide AI artifacts. |
| 3. AI cinematic mystery/"lost civilization" series | ~$6–12 | Recurring characters signal originality. Must avoid fake news and fake disasters. |
| 4. Hindi/Hinglish mythology and itihaas storytelling | ~10x below English | Fast reach, but low pay and close to slop formats. |
| 5. Narrated finance/AI-tools explainers | $10–30+ | Highest pay, but crowded, and AI-persona monetization is banned. Only worth it with real expertise. |

Sources: RPMs from [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/he-made-$300k-from-videos-people-fall-asleep-to-inside-youtubes-ai-slop-economy) and [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/how-much-do-youtubers-make/). Zack D. Films figure via [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-channels).

Treat every RPM figure above as a rough range. No large independent dataset of RPM by niche exists, and many published numbers come from tool vendors with no stated method.

### The Hindi question is mostly a math question

The case for Hindi is real: reach comes easily, and the high-quality long-form tier is thinner. The pay gap is too wide to start there, though. India-audience long-form RPM runs **₹40–120 for education** and **₹80–250 for finance**. The same finance content watched by US viewers earns about **₹650–3,300 RPM**, and **English content in India earns 2–5x the CPM of Hindi in the same niche** ([IdentityKit](https://www.identitykit.in/blog/youtube-rpm-india-niche-2026)). Hindi CPMs sit around **₹25–80, against ₹80–250 for English**, and mass Hindi entertainment falls to ₹8–30 ([UpGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-in-india-complete-guide/)).

The biggest Indian AI success story is also a warning. **Bandar Apna Dost**, which posts AI videos of a realistic monkey in human situations, reached **2.07B views and an estimated $4.25M a year**. Kapwing's study of 15,000 top channels nonetheless classifies it as AI slop ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/)). That is not a model a new creator can safely copy.

A cheaper hedge exists. Since 4 Feb 2026, **auto-dubbing in 27 languages is available to all creators** ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-algorithm-updates/)). An English channel can therefore test Hindi demand without building a second channel. If Hindi audio tracks pull real watch time, a Hinglish channel can follow later. Hinglish keeps much of both CPM pools ([UpGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-in-india-complete-guide/)).

### Long-form earns the money, Shorts bring the viewers

Shorts pay roughly **$0.01–0.08 RPM**, out of a pooled fund with a **45% creator share**, against **55% on long-form** ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-monetization/)). YouTube's own growth director says viewers watching on TVs want longer videos, so the system recommends them more. He also says subscribers click only **"about maybe a 10% or less"** of the uploads in their feed. And for a new channel with no viewer history, the system matches the video's topic to interested viewers; if nobody responds, it stops showing the video ([PPC Land, quoting Todd Beaupré](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)). That argues for one tight topic, question-style titles, and series that chain into each other through suggested videos. Since 1 Oct 2026, original Shorts also rank above re-uploads and aggregation ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-algorithm-updates/)). Cut your Shorts from your own long-form, never from other people's videos.

## YouTube pays for AI work that is clearly authored, not templated

YouTube does not ban AI. Since **15 Jul 2025**, its monetization policy has called "inauthentic content" anything that is **"AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective."** The same policy explicitly allows a reused intro and outro, and **"a series following a set of characters across episodes"** as long as each video has a distinct storyline. Violations are judged across the whole channel, not video by video ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).

In mid-July 2026, YouTube's Trust & Safety chief Matt Halprin named three categories that cannot be monetized ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)):

| Category | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Generic, repetitive or template content | Videos that are "very generic and don't really have a narrative arc" |
| Unsatisfying or off-putting content | Manipulative formulas such as staged animal "rescues", with or without AI |
| AI personas on sensitive topics | A synthetic presenter posing as a human expert on finance, legal or health topics |

Halprin's line, AI-made videos that are "very generic and don't really have a narrative arc", is the test a history channel has to pass on every upload.

Enforcement turned real in 2025–26. **Screen Culture and KH Studio**, two AI fake-trailer channels, were terminated in December 2025. A January 2026 wave removed or wiped about **16 channels with roughly 35M subscribers and 4.7B views**, including AI Bible-story and AI animal channels with 5–6M subscribers each ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/)). A reported Google system for terminating clusters of channels flags networks by "synced upload schedules, templated formats, and common production infrastructure" ([Bottle Rocket Content](https://www.bottlerocketcontent.com/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-scale-revealed/)). That report is unverified. Its practical message still stands: do not run several near-identical sister channels from one template.

Human-made faceless channels also report being caught in the crackdown ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)), so visible signs of authorship matter. The most useful ones are a consistent narrative voice, sourced claims and video structures that vary from upload to upload. Accuracy matters too. The AI time-traveller history channel *Chloe vs History* (259K subscribers) was publicly fact-checked for anachronisms and gibberish Latin ([Knowledge Lust](https://samuelrinko.substack.com/p/the-ai-youtube-channel-that-teaches)). On-screen citations are both a quality signal and a defence.

### Disclose photorealistic AI: it costs nothing

You must tick **"AI use: Yes"** in YouTube Studio when a video creates "a realistic scene that didn't actually occur", alters real events or places, or makes real people appear to do things they didn't. Animated or clearly unrealistic visuals are exempt, and so are scripts, thumbnails and upscaling. YouTube states plainly that disclosure **"won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money"**. Repeatedly failing to disclose can lead to removal or suspension from YPP ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Since **27 May 2026**, YouTube has also applied the label itself when it detects undeclared photorealistic AI ([MiniMatters](https://minimatters.com/youtube-ai-content-labeling-update-in-may-2026/)).

Photorealistic history reconstructions therefore need disclosure. For creators in India, India's IT Amendment Rules 2026 (in force since **20 Feb 2026**) separately require platforms to collect and verify uploader declarations for "synthetically generated information" ([Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/india-targets-deepfakes-and-ai-generated-content-key-changes-under-meitys-2026-102mjwn)). Both rules point the same way: always declare. Never use a real person's AI face or voice. Real people can have such content removed through YouTube's privacy process ([Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/you-can-now-make-takedown-requests-for-ai-generated-youtube-videos-that-mimic-your-likeness)).

### The YPP bar doubles on 1 Feb 2027

Today, full YPP (ad revenue) needs **1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in 12 months, or 10M Shorts views in 90 days**. Shorts views do not count toward watch hours ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)). For new applicants from **1 Feb 2027**, the bar becomes **8,000 hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views in 90 days**. Creators will also need **10M Shorts views per 90 days** to earn from the Shorts pool in a given month. Existing partners are unaffected ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)).

The lower "expanded" tier (500 subscribers, 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views) unlocks fan funding but not ad revenue. India is eligible for it ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240)).

To see what these bars mean in views: 4,000 hours is 240,000 minutes, or about **48,000 long-form views at a 5-minute average view duration**. Hitting that by 31 Jan 2027 is ambitious but not absurd for a strong history series. Plan for the 8,000-hour bar (about 96,000 such views) and treat early qualification as upside.

## Wan2GP is a free b-roll studio on a rented GPU

WanGP is a free, open-source Gradio web app by the developer DeepBeepMeep. It bills itself as a "one-stop super app for the best open source generative models across video, image, audio, and text-to-speech", built for the "GPU Poor" ([README](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/blob/main/README.md)). It ships **35+ dated releases in nine months of 2026** ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/blob/main/docs/CHANGELOG.md)). New open models usually arrive quickly and with low-VRAM optimizations. The flip side is that the docs lag the code, and an upgrade sometimes means reinstalling acceleration kernels.

WanGP supports:

| Type | Models |
|---|---|
| Video | Wan 2.1/2.2 and derivatives, MiniMax H3, LTX-2/2.3/2.5, HunyuanVideo 1/1.5, LongCat, Kandinsky 5, MagiHuman |
| Image | Krea 2, Qwen Image, Z-Image, Flux 1/2, Ideogram 4 |
| Voice and music | Qwen3 TTS, IndexTTS2, Chatterbox, Ace Step, YuE2, Stable Audio 3 |

Its features include image-to-video, first/last-frame control, sliding windows for longer clips, LoRAs, upscalers and a headless batch queue ([README](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/blob/main/README.md)).

It does **not** run Wan 2.5, 2.6, 2.7 or 3.0. Alibaba's open-weight line stops at Wan 2.2 ([howaiworks.ai](https://howaiworks.ai/blog/alibaba-wan-open-weights-stopped-at-2-2)). Wan 3.0 is API-only at **$0.20 per output second**, about $12 per minute ([TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/alibaba-wan3-video-model-after-share-sale)).

Quality has caught up with closed tools more than most people assume. On the Artificial Analysis text-to-video leaderboard, open-weight **MiniMax H3 ranks #4 (Elo 1138)**, above closed Seedance 2.0 (1118), Kling 3.0 (1016) and Veo 3.1 (966). LTX-2.5 sits lower, at about 945 ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video)). The board changes often.

For a history channel, the most useful models in WanGP are:

| Model | Use | VRAM / speed (developer figures unless noted) |
|---|---|---|
| **Wan 2.2 image-to-video + Lightning LoRAs** | Mature workhorse with a large LoRA ecosystem | About 1–3 min per 5 s 480p clip on an RTX 4090 ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/what-is-wan-2-2-video-open-source)) |
| **LTX-2.3 distilled** | Speed and native audio | 20 s at 720p in about 2 min on 24 GB; 10 s at 720p needs only 8 GB VRAM ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/blob/main/docs/CHANGELOG.md)) |
| **MiniMax H3** | Hero shots | Needs 15–20 steps; generate at 480p and upscale ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/blob/main/docs/CHANGELOG.md)) |

The real hardware constraint is **system RAM**: 32 GB is the practical minimum and 64 GB avoids LoRA out-of-memory crashes ([runaihome.com](https://runaihome.com/blog/wan-video-local-ai-gpu-guide-2026/); [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/blob/main/docs/CHANGELOG.md)).

### Limitations you have to design around

Clips are short: a native window is about 5 s for Wan, 15 s for H3 at 480p and 20 s for LTX-2. Longer clips drift. The official docs warn that "artifacts can accumulate and character identity may progressively drift" ([PROCESSING.md](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/blob/main/docs/PROCESSING.md)). Video models are also poor at rendering text, and the docs steer you to image models for typography ([MODELS.md](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/blob/main/docs/MODELS.md)).

The practical answer is to cut between 5–15 second shots rather than chase long continuous takes. Generate a reference image first and drive image-to-video from it for consistency. Put all titles, maps and dates in the editor.

## Only some Wan2GP models are safe to monetize

WanGP's own **Community License 2.0** lets you "use, reproduce, publish… sell, and license Outputs for any lawful purpose." A "Made with WanGP" credit is required only for "Direct Output Sales", meaning selling the clips themselves. The license's ban on "ad-supported" use applies to offering the *software* as a service, not to your videos ([LICENSE.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/main/LICENSE.txt)). Ad-monetized YouTube videos are therefore fine; a one-line credit in the description costs nothing. Each model's own license applies on top of WanGP's.

| Model | License | OK for a monetized channel? |
|---|---|---|
| **Wan 2.1 / 2.2** | Apache 2.0 | **Yes**, the default ([howaiworks.ai](https://howaiworks.ai/blog/alibaba-wan-open-weights-stopped-at-2-2.md)) |
| **LTX-2 / 2.x** | LTX-2 Community License | **Yes**, below $10M annual revenue; the licensor "claims no rights in the Output" ([LTX-2 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2/blob/main/LICENSE)). Only the LTX-2 license was checked; 2.3/2.5 are assumed to match |
| HunyuanVideo 1.5 | Tencent Community License | Not if you are in the **EU, UK or South Korea**. Elsewhere, machine-generated content must be "expressly and conspicuously" identified ([Hunyuan LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5/blob/main/LICENSE)) |
| MiniMax H3 | H3 Community License | Not if you are in the **USA, EU, UK or South Korea**. Above $20M revenue you need authorization. Output terms are not fully verified ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/minimax-ships-h3-weights-with-the-us-and-eu-excluded/)) |
| Z-Image, Qwen-Image, FLUX.1 schnell, FLUX.2 klein-4B | Apache 2.0 | **Yes** ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/open-source-image-models-licenses/)) |
| FLUX.1 [dev] | Non-commercial weights | Outputs may be used commercially ([FLUX.1-dev LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-dev/blob/main/LICENSE.md)) |
| Ideogram 4 | Gated non-commercial | **Avoid** ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/open-source-image-models-licenses/)) |
| Chatterbox | MIT, with an embedded watermark; Hindi pack available | **Yes** ([Resemble AI](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/chatterbox-multilingual-v3-tts-with-embedded-watermarking-for-25-languages)) |
| Qwen3-TTS | Apache 2.0, 10 languages, **no Hindi** | Yes, English only ([QwenLM](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS)) |
| IndexTTS2 | Weights license disputed | **Avoid** until clarified ([index-tts #228](https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts/issues/228)) |

The territory clauses matter because the brief does not say where you live. A creator in India can use every model in the table. A US-based creator must drop H3, and an EU or UK creator must drop H3 and Hunyuan as well. **Wan 2.2 plus LTX-2.x is safe almost everywhere** and should be the default stack.

Ownership has a catch. Licensors claim no rights in outputs, but in the US purely AI-generated material is not copyrightable. The Supreme Court declined to hear *Thaler v. Perlmutter* on 2 Mar 2026, leaving the human-authorship rule in place ([Munck Wilson](https://www.munckwilson.com/news-insights/supreme-court-denies-certiorari-in-thaler-v-perlmutter-human-authorship-still-required/)). Anyone can reuse your raw clips. What you own is the human layer: script, narration choices, edit, selection and arrangement. That is one more reason to invest there.

## A one-video-a-week pipeline costs about $30–60 a month

The workflow below keeps true AI motion as seasoning, about **10–20% of runtime**. AI stills with slow pans and zooms carry roughly half the video, and maps and graphics fill the rest. This mix is a practitioner norm rather than a sourced rule. It keeps GPU cost low and limits how much drift and artifacting viewers see.

For voice, ElevenLabs Starter (about **$5–6/month**) includes a commercial license and 30,000 credits. That covers about three 1,500-word scripts. Creator (**$22/month**) covers four long videos plus Shorts ([smallest.ai](https://smallest.ai/blog/elevenlabs-pricing-plans-cost-what-you-get-in-2026)). Free and commercially safe alternatives are **Kokoro** (Apache 2.0, includes Hindi) ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/kokoro/)) and **Chatterbox**. Avoid **F5-TTS** (non-commercial weights) and **XTTS-v2** (non-commercial, and its company has shut down, so no commercial license can be bought) ([localaimaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/f5-tts-setup-guide); [localaimaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/xtts-coqui-commercial-license)). For a later Hindi track, **Sarvam Bulbul v3** costs about **₹3 per 1,000 characters**, under ₹30 per long script ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/sarvam-bulbul-indian-tts/)). **Indic Parler-TTS** is a free Apache-licensed option ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts)).

Edit in DaVinci Resolve (free). If you are in India, note that **CapCut has been banned since June 2020** ([Kripesh Adwani](https://kripeshadwani.com/how-to-use-capcut-in-india/)).

| Stage | Tooling | Est. time/week |
|---|---|---|
| Topic research | YouTube autocomplete, Studio Research tab, Google Trends | 1–2 h |
| Script (1,200–2,500 words) | LLM outline, then 3–5 primary sources, a fact-check of every number, and a human rewrite with a point of view | 3–5 h |
| Voice | ElevenLabs Starter/Creator, or Kokoro/Chatterbox | 0.5–1.5 h |
| Stills | Z-Image / Qwen-Image / FLUX.2 klein-4B in WanGP on the rented pod | 1–2 h |
| AI motion | Wan 2.2 image-to-video + Lightning, or LTX-2.3 distilled, run as a batch queue on a 4090 | 2–4 h |
| Edit, captions, music | Resolve; YouTube Audio Library for music; on-screen citations | 4–6 h |
| 2–4 Shorts | Vertical cuts of the strongest beats | 1.5–3 h |
| Thumbnail, title, upload | Canva/Photopea; tick "AI use: Yes" for photorealistic scenes; "Made with WanGP" credit | 1–1.5 h |
| **Total** | **≈$5–15/mo GPU only, or ≈$30–60/mo with paid voice and tools** | **≈14–25 h** |

The time and cost figures in this table are estimates.

## The first 90 days: launch before the bar doubles

The plan runs from today (3 Oct 2026) to early January 2027. That window catches the Q4 advertising season, when CPMs run **40–80% above Q1** ([UpGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-in-india-complete-guide/)). It also leaves about four weeks of runway before the 1 Feb 2027 YPP change. The cadence is deliberately human-scale: one long-form video and 2–4 Shorts per week. That matches the effort a reviewer would expect from one person and avoids the volume pattern behind the cluster terminations.

| Window | Goals | Concrete deliverables |
|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–14** (Oct 3–16) | Lock the identity and prove the pipeline | Pick one narrow lane, for example "What if…" counterfactual history or "the real story behind…". Define a series name, visual style guide, narrator voice and citation style. List 30 question-style titles. Set up RunPod with WanGP on a network volume and do a one-day test of Wan 2.2 image-to-video and LTX-2.3. Choose a voice (ElevenLabs Starter or Kokoro). Set up 2-step verification and channel basics. Produce **two complete pilot videos** and do not publish them yet. |
| **Days 15–42** (Oct 17–Nov 13) | Launch and learn the topic match | Publish pilot 1 and pilot 2 a week apart, then one new long-form every week (about 5 by day 42). Publish 2–4 Shorts per long-form, each ending with a pointer to the full video. Build playlists so videos chain as a series. Disclose AI on every photorealistic video. Turn on auto-dubbing. |
| **Days 43–70** (Nov 14–Dec 11) | Double down on what YouTube is testing well | In Studio, find the 2–3 topics with the best click-through rate and average view duration, and commission follow-ups and part 2s on them. Vary the structure (countdown, single-narrative, debate) so no two videos look templated. Refresh weak thumbnails. Batch next month's AI clips in a single pod session. |
| **Days 71–90** (Dec 12–Jan 1) | Decision gates | **YPP gate:** if the channel is on pace for 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 hours by 31 Jan 2027, push extra long-form; otherwise re-plan for 8,000 hours. **Hindi gate:** if dubbed Hindi tracks pull meaningful watch time, scope a Hinglish channel with its *own* format and workflow, not a clone. **Cost gate:** keep GPU spend under about $15/month. If keep-rates are poor, shift toward stills and LTX. |

Do not touch, at any stage: AI "expert" personas on money, health or law; fake trailers, fake news or fabricated "true stories"; AI animal or "rescue" content; sleep and horror slideshow mass uploads; any real person's AI face or voice; and sister channels that share one template.

## Conclusion

Two things decide whether a faceless AI channel succeeds in late 2026. The tools are now cheap: rented 4090s cost cents per clip, and an open-weight model sits near the top of the text-to-video leaderboard. What decides success is how clearly a reviewer, and the satisfaction-driven recommendation system, can tell that a person authored each video. That turns the usual AI-channel playbook upside down. Volume, which built the 2024–25 slop empires, is now the main liability. Research depth, on-screen sourcing and a recurring series identity are cheap for a careful solo creator and hard for a content farm to fake. **Recommendation:** launch an English-language, research-led history and "what if" mini-documentary series. Build it with Wan 2.2 and LTX-2.x inside WanGP on a Community Cloud 4090, Apache-licensed image models and a commercial-safe voice. Disclose photorealistic AI every time, and treat Hindi as a dubbing experiment until the data justifies a separate channel.

Some uncertainties remain. No reliable timing benchmarks for WanGP exist for H3 or LTX-2.5. Niche RPM figures come largely from vendor estimates. The MiniMax H3 license and some newer model licenses (Krea 2, Qwen Image 2.1) were not fully verified. The reported Google cluster-termination paper is unconfirmed. Re-check WanGP's model list and each model card before committing a production stack, since both change every couple of weeks.
